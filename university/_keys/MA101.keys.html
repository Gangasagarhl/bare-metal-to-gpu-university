Final question F23 — forensic answer (14 points)

Evidence: the paper shows university/labs/MA101-P/teams_bug.out exactly as the Lab Engineer's run printed it.

  1. Observation (2). "step 3: jobs never done: 48 49" and "step 4: jobs done: 48 of 50, idle helpers: 0". Two of the 50 jobs (numbered 0 to 49) were never done, and no helper was idle, so there were fewer helpers than jobs.
  2. Hypotheses (4; 2 per plausible hypothesis, at least two needed). A: the number of teams was rounded down instead of up, so there were too few helpers. B: the ticket formula skipped or repeated some tickets (for example seat and team swapped), so two jobs got no helper although enough helpers came. C: the bounds check stopped tickets 48 and 49 (for example ticket < 48).
  3. Evidence-based conclusion (4). Step 1 prints "teams = 50 / 8 = 6" and step 2 "helpers = 6 x 8 = 48": 48 helpers can never do 50 jobs, whatever the tickets or the check do; and "idle helpers: 0" shows that every helper worked, so B and C are ruled out (a formula or check fault with enough helpers would leave idle helpers or repeats). Root cause: step 1 used plain whole-number division, 50 ÷ 8 = 6 (rounded down), where the rounding-up formula (50 + 8 − 1) ÷ 8 = 7 was needed. The jobs never done are exactly 48 and 49, the ones past the last helper's ticket 47, which confirms it.
  4. Fix (2). Step 1: teams = (50 + 8 − 1) ÷ 8 = 57 ÷ 8 = 7 (whole-number division). Step 2: helpers = 7 × 8 = 56. The fixed program prints "jobs done: 50 of 50, idle helpers: 6". Verification: jobs done equals jobs, and working + idle = 56.
  5. Prevention (2). After planning, check teams × teamSize ≥ jobs and stop with a message if not; or check at the end that jobs done equals jobs. Test the plan with a small case that does not divide exactly (10 jobs, teams of 4 → 3 teams, F0-19).

How the evidence was produced. teams_bug.cpp is the F0-19 Listing 1 plan with int teams = jobs / teamSize; in place of (jobs + teamSize - 1) / teamSize, for 50 jobs and teams of 8; it prints the undone jobs and was run with the runner (exit code 0; the sanitizers stayed quiet because the bounds check is correct). Wrong answers and why: "the bounds check is wrong" — tickets 0 to 47 all worked and none was idle, so the check let every existing ticket through; "the ticket formula is wrong" — the tickets 0 to 47 are each used once (48 jobs done by 48 helpers); "two helpers were lazy" — a program's helpers do exactly what the code says.
