Scope

Notes and quotes (paraphrased unless quoted)

  • UM10204 §3.1.6: "No receiver is present on the bus with the transmitted address so there is no device to respond with an acknowledge" (one of the causes of a NACK); §3.1.10: the controller-receiver sends a not-acknowledge before the STOP. Speed modes: Standard-mode 100 kbit/s, Fast-mode 400 kbit/s, Fast-mode Plus 1 Mbit/s, High-speed mode 3.4 Mbit/s.
  • LSM6DSOX DS12814 Rev 4: address 110101x (SA0 low 0x6A, high 0x6B); WHO_AM_I 0Fh = 6Ch; CTRL1_XL (10h) ±2/4/8/16 g; CTRL2_G (11h) ±125/250/500/1000/2000 dps; OUTX_L_A 28h, OUTX_H_A 29h, two's complement; IF_INC in CTRL3_C (12h) for address auto-increment; CTRL1_XL and CTRL2_G default 00h (power-down); VDD 1.71–3.6 V; sensitivity 0.061 mg/LSB at ±2 g, 8.75 mdps/LSB at ±250 dps. Block data update was not in the read excerpt.
  • Consistency check: 2 g / 32768 = 0.0610 mg per count, which matches the chapter's "range / 32768" rule; the LSM6DSOX stores the low byte first, the opposite of the invented U-IMU6, and the chapter now says so.

Fact list (keyed to the chapter's claim tags, in chapter order)

  1. F1 (jargon) … alogy: Lucía's wrist and her sense of turning while she carries the soup. Precise: a sensor package that measures specific force with accelerometers and angular rate with gyroscopes, usually along three perpendicular axes each; a magnetometer is often added. — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  2. F2 (jargon) … avity pulling down. Analogy: the weight of the bowl shifting in Lucía's wrist. Precise: a sensor whose output is the specific force along its axis: acceleration minus gravitational acceleration, so at rest it reads 1 g directed opposite to gravity (upwards). — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  3. F3 (jargon) Simple words: what an accelerometer really feels: every push except gravity's own pull. Analogy: the table pushing up on the bowl when it rests. Precise: the non-gravitational force per unit mass, f = a − g (vectors), in m/s² or in units of g. — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  4. F4 (jargon) Simple words: a sensor that measures how fast it is turning. Analogy: Lucía feeling herself turn the corner. Precise: a sensor whose output is the angular rate about its axis, in degrees or radians per second; MEMS gyros used in IMUs measure rate, not angle. — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  5. F5 (jargon) … nalogy: seeing the sunlit courtyard and knowing which way you face. Precise: a sensor that measures the local magnetic field vector; heading can be estimated from its horizontal components once tilt is known, and nearby iron and electric currents disturb it. — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  6. F6 (jargon) Simple words: a sensor's reading when the true value is zero. Analogy: a kitchen scale that shows 3 grams with nothing on it. Precise: a constant or slowly varying offset added to a sensor's output; it is estimated (calibrated) and subtracted. — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  7. F7 (jargon) … inning slowly for a long time, Lucía no longer knows which way she faces. Precise: the growth of error in an angle obtained by integrating rate measurements; a constant bias b produces an error b·t that grows linearly with time, and noise adds a random walk. — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  8. F8 (jargon) Simple words: the largest value the sensor can report before it saturates. Analogy: the highest mark on the scale's dial. Precise: the configured measurement range (for example ±4 g); with a 16-bit signed output, one count is range / 32768. — source: D4 LSM6DSOX DS12814 Rev 4
  9. F9 (jargon) , k = 0 … N−1; bin k corresponds to k·fs/N hertz; the FFT is a fast algorithm that computes the same values. — source: D2 O&S 3rd ed. ch. 8–10 (contents only)
  10. F10 (jargon) Simple words: a chart of "how big is each frequency" in a signal. Analogy: a list of each kitchen machine and how loud it is. Precise: for a real signal, A[k] = 2|X[k]|/N for 0 < k < N/2 gives the amplitude of a sinusoid at bin frequency k·fs/N. — source: D2 O&S 3rd ed. ch. 8–10 (contents only)
  11. F11 (fig1) Figure 1. The three sensors of an IMU and the main weakness of each. Axis directions and sign conventions differ between parts: the chip's datasheet defines them (D4). — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  12. F12 (fig2) Figure 2. A register burst read on I2C, at byte level (bit timing is not shown). The framing (start, 7-bit address plus read/write bit, acknowledge, repeated start, not-acknowledge before stop) follows the I2C-bus specification (D3); the values are from R1. — source: D3 UM10204 Rev 7.0 §3.1.4–3.1.10
  13. F13 (l2-acc) … s weight, and also every push she gives it. An accelerometer measures specific force: the vector f = a − g, where a is the sensor's acceleration and g is gravitational acceleration (pointing down). At rest, a = 0, so f = −g: a vector of size 1 g pointing up. — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  14. F14 (l2-acc) … sensor's z axis is up when it lies level, a level resting sensor reads (0, 0, +1) g. Tilted by roll φ about x and pitch θ about y, it reads (−sin θ, sin φ cos θ, cos φ cos θ) g, the formula used inside the university's pretend IMU (line 103 of uimu_model.h). — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  15. F15 (l2-acc) Listing 1 recovers roll 30.07° and pitch −9.99° for a model tilted by 30° and −10°, and the length of the averaged vector is 0.9984 g. — source: R1 (local run, labs/F1-65)
  16. F16 (l2-acc) The formula holds only when a = 0. A robot that accelerates, bumps or vibrates adds a to f, and the "tilt" computed this way is wrong while that lasts. — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  17. F17 (l2-gyro) The gyroscope outputs angular rate ω about each axis, plus a bias b and noise. — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  18. F18 (l2-gyro) … g 2 integrates a gyro that is not turning at all, with a pretend bias of 0.10 °/s: after 60 s the angle reads 5.964°, close to 0.10 × 60 = 6°. With the bias subtracted, the error after 60 s is −0.036°: the noise's random walk, much smaller but still growing. — source: R2 (local run, labs/F1-65)
  19. F19 (l2-gyro) Calibration estimates b by averaging while the sensor is known to be still; Listing 1's 200-sample average gives +0.803, −0.349, +0.098 °/s for a model bias of +0.80, −0.35, +0.10. — source: R1 (local run, labs/F1-65)
  20. F20 (l2-gyro) Real biases change with temperature and time, which is why estimators keep re-estimating them (RB302, DN301). — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  21. F21 (l2-mag) The magnetometer measures the local magnetic field vector. Once tilt is known from the accelerometer, the field can be projected onto the horizontal plane and a heading computed. — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  22. F22 (l2-mag) The field near a robot is distorted by magnetised parts and by iron that bends the field, and by the currents in motor wires; calibration (and placing the sensor away from motors) reduces but does not remove this. — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  23. F23 (l2-regs) Most IMU chips contain the whole chain of F1-64 for each axis and present results in registers read over I2C or SPI (F1-47). — source: D4 LSM6DSOX DS12814 Rev 4
  24. F24 (l2-regs) … full-scale range and data rate), read (a burst of data registers) and convert (bytes → signed counts → units). The university's pretend part, U-IMU6, stores each axis as a 16-bit two's-complement number, high byte first; with range ±R, one count is R/32768. — source: R1 (local run, labs/F1-65)
  25. F25 (l2-regs) In the run, the z bytes 1B 6D form 0x1B6D = 7021 counts = 7021 × 4 / 32768 = 0.8571 g at ±4 g. Swapping the two bytes by mistake gives 27931 counts and an impossible 3.41 g. — source: R1 (local run, labs/F1-65)
  26. F26 (l2-regs) Which byte comes first, whether the values are signed, the scale factors and the axis directions are defined only by the real part's datasheet. — source: D4 LSM6DSOX DS12814 Rev 4
  27. F27 (l2-spectrum) Motors and wheels shake the robot, and the accelerometer feels every shake. In a time plot this looks like "noise". A DFT splits a block of N samples into frequency bins k·fs/N; the amplitude spectrum 2|X[k]|/N shows how big a sinusoid at each bin is. — source: D2 O&S 3rd ed. ch. 8–10 (contents only)
  28. F28 (l2-spectrum) Random sensor noise spreads thinly over all bins; vibration from a rotating part piles up at its rotation frequency and its multiples (harmonics). — source: D2 O&S 3rd ed. ch. 8–10 (contents only)
  29. F29 (layer3) … asure the displacement of a small proof mass on springs, usually by capacitance; MEMS gyros measure the Coriolis effect on a vibrating mass. Both have resonances, and vibration near those or above the output data rate can alias into the band you use (F1-64). — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  30. F30 (layer3) Datasheets specify internal digital low-pass filters and output data rates; choose them together. — source: D4 LSM6DSOX DS12814 Rev 4
  31. F31 (layer3) … dard per-axis model is measurement = scale × true + bias + noise, plus cross-axis coupling and misalignment; full calibration estimates scale, bias and alignment from several known orientations (for an accelerometer, the six faces of a cube against gravity). — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  32. F32 (layer3) Clipping. Large vibration can exceed the full-scale range; clipped samples cannot be averaged back. A clipped sine's average is no longer zero, so clipping also shifts the mean and corrupts tilt. Choose a range with headroom for the vibration you measure. — source: R1 (local run, labs/F1-65)
  33. F33 (layer3) … 000 samples at 1000 Hz give 1 Hz bins (Listing 3). A frequency between two bins spreads into neighbours ("leakage"); a window function reduces that at the cost of wider peaks. The direct DFT costs N² operations; an FFT gets the same numbers in about N log N. — source: D2 O&S 3rd ed. ch. 8–10 (contents only)
  34. F34 (layer3) numpy's FFT on the same file gives the same four peaks to four decimals (R5), which is the cross-check of Listing 3. — source: R5 (local run, labs/F1-65)
  35. F35 (layer3) I2C details. I2C transfers are framed by start and stop conditions; each byte is acknowledged by the receiver; the controller reading data acknowledges each byte except the last; a device that does not acknowledge its address signals absence. — source: D3 UM10204 Rev 7.0 §3.1.4–3.1.10
  36. F36 (hardware) … ions, shifts bytes out and in on the data line while it drives the clock line, and reports events (address acknowledged, byte received, no acknowledge) through status flags and interrupts; your driver feeds it bytes and reacts to those events (F1-47, F1-42). — source: D3 UM10204 Rev 7.0 §3.1.4–3.1.10
  37. F37 (hardware) On the IMU side, the converter samples each axis at the configured output data rate and latches results into the data registers; many parts guarantee that a burst read returns all axes from the same sample. — source: D4 LSM6DSOX DS12814 Rev 4
  38. F38 (hardware) The pretend U-IMU6 does this explicitly: reading the first data register latches a fresh sample (line 78 of the model). — source: R1 (local run, labs/F1-65)
  39. F39 (hardware) … (their data-rate fields default to 0) and must be switched on; and the stated sensitivity, 0.061 mg per count at ±2 g and 8.75 m°/s per count at ±250 °/s, is what the "range / 32768" rule of Layer 2 gives (2 g / 32768 = 0.0610 mg). The supply is 1.71–3.6 V. — source: D4 LSM6DSOX DS12814 Rev 4
  40. F40 (worked) Sanity check. |a| = √(0.2291² + 0.4990² + 0.8571²) = √(0.0525 + 0.2490 + 0.7346) = √1.0361 = 1.018 g: one noisy sample of a resting sensor, close to 1 g. The 200-sample average in step 5 of the output is 0.9984 g. — source: R1 (local run, labs/F1-65)
  41. F41 (breaks) Lucía knows when she is pushing; the accelerometer does not. She can tell her own push from a tilt. The accelerometer measures their sum, f = a − g, and cannot separate them without other information. — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  42. F42 (breaks) Lucía's drift is vague; a gyro's is arithmetic. A bias of b degrees per second produces exactly b·t degrees of error, as Listing 2 shows (5.964° after 60 s for 0.10 °/s). — source: R2 (local run, labs/F1-65)
  43. F43 (breaks) The courtyard is always where it is; the magnetic field near a robot is not. Motor currents and steel parts change the field the magnetometer sees, sometimes more than the Earth's field itself; seeing the courtyard is never disturbed by the soup. — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)
  44. F44 (transition) Where you came from: F1-64 built the measurement chain; F1-47 introduced I2C and SPI; MA201 gave vectors and rotations. What carries over: codes, step sizes and sampling rules; register reads over a bus; two's-complement numbers (MA102). — source: D3 UM10204 Rev 7.0 §3.1.4–3.1.10
  45. F45 (transition) … by registers you write; measurements are three-dimensional vectors in the sensor's own axes; gravity is always in the accelerometer's reading; integrating a rate turns small biases into growing errors; and vibration must be looked at in the frequency domain. — source: D1 Beard & McLain 2012 (publisher page only; text not opened)

If the IMU is mounted on a robot with motors, the motors are disconnected or the robot is clamped with wheels off the ground. No propellers. — source: S1 guide (local)
