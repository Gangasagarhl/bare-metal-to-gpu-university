Fact list (keyed to the chapter's claim tags, in chapter order)

  1. F1 (jargon) … the thing you want to measure is compared with the wobble on top of it. Analogy: the soup level compared with the sloshing. Precise: the ratio of signal power to noise power, often given in decibels as 10·log10(Ps/Pn), or as a ratio of amplitudes (20·log10). — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. §8.1/§8.15/§8.16/§5.13–5.17 (TOC only)
  2. F2 (jargon) … wobble smaller, but slowly. Analogy: Kenji's several dipstick readings. Precise: the mean of N independent readings, each with standard deviation σ, has standard deviation σ/√N; averaging does not reduce offsets or noise that is correlated between readings. — source: D4 Blitzstein & Hwang (not opened)
  3. F3 (jargon) … ay fast wobbles. Analogy: Kenji writing down a value that only moves a tenth of the way towards each new reading. Precise: the first-order recursive filter y[n] = y[n−1] + a·(x[n] − y[n−1]), 0 < a ≤ 1; its gain falls with frequency, and it delays the signal. — source: D3 O&S 3rd ed. ch. 5–7 (contents only)
  4. F4 (jargon) … : the frequency at which a filter starts to cut a signal noticeably. Analogy: how quickly the soup can really change before Kenji's slow log starts missing it. Precise: the frequency at which the filter's amplitude gain is 1/√2 (−3 dB) of its pass-band gain. — source: D3 O&S 3rd ed. ch. 5–7 (contents only)
  5. F5 (jargon) … ees. Analogy: the shared drain pipe backing up under the soup pot. Precise: interference caused by a current I1 flowing through an impedance Z also in circuit 2's path, adding I1·Z to circuit 2's signal; common in ground returns shared by motors and sensors. — source: D2 Ott (not opened)
  6. F6 (jargon) … wn pipe to the main sewer. Precise: a grounding layout in which separate return paths meet at a single point, so that one circuit's return current does not flow through another's reference; at high frequency, ground planes and short return loops matter more. — source: D2 Ott (not opened)
  7. F7 (jargon) … water beside each sink for sudden needs. Precise: a capacitor placed close to a device's supply pins, providing a low-impedance local source for fast current changes and keeping supply noise from spreading; values and placement are in the device's datasheet. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. §8.1/§8.15/§8.16/§5.13–5.17 (TOC only)
  8. F8 (jargon) … rn pipes side by side. Precise: twisting a signal and its return wire together keeps the loop area small and cancels much magnetic pickup; a shield (a conductive cover) intercepts electric-field coupling and must be connected at the end the design specifies. — source: D2 Ott (not opened)
  9. F9 (fig1) Figure 1. The wiring of the forensic robot (R3, R4), simplified. Left: motor and sensor returns share 0.05 Ω (pretend). Right: separate returns to one point. — source: D2 Ott (not opened)
  10. F10 (fig2) Figure 2. Measured gain of the a = 0.1 filter at 1000 samples/s (R2). It passes slow changes, cuts the F1-65 vibration frequencies to about a fifth and a tenth, and delays a step by about 10 ms. — source: R2 (local run, labs/F1-72)
  11. F11 (l2-avg) If readings x1…xN are independent with standard deviation σ, their mean has standard deviation σ/√N. — source: D4 Blitzstein & Hwang (not opened)
  12. F12 (l2-avg) Listing 1 checks this with a synthetic 1.000 V signal and 10 mV of noise: measured spreads 10.207, 5.121, 2.445, 1.247 and 0.624 mV for N = 1, 4, 16, 64, 256, against predictions 10, 5, 2.5, 1.25 and 0.625 mV. — source: R1 (local run, labs/F1-72)
  13. F13 (l2-avg) Two limits matter. First, cost: each halving of the noise needs four times the readings, so four times the time. Second, an offset is not noise: with a 25 mV offset, the 256-reading average is 1.0249 V, still about 25 mV high. — source: R1 (local run, labs/F1-72)
  14. F14 (l2-avg) Averaging also assumes independence; noise that changes slowly (drift) or follows another signal (interference) is correlated and averages away much less. — source: D4 Blitzstein & Hwang (not opened)
  15. F15 (l2-filter) A running average throws away nothing new and smooths continuously. The simplest recursive filter is the exponential moving average: y[n] = y[n−1] + a·(x[n] − y[n−1]). — source: D3 O&S 3rd ed. ch. 5–7 (contents only)
  16. F16 (l2-filter) |: 0.9982 at 1 Hz, 0.7026 at 17 Hz (−3.07 dB, so the cut-off is near 17 Hz), 0.1916 at 87 Hz, 0.1009 at 174 Hz. — source: R2 (local run, labs/F1-72)
  17. F17 (l2-filter) At 50 Hz the measured peak is 0.3157 against 0.3193: at 1000 samples/s, the 50 Hz wave's samples do not land exactly on its peaks, so the largest sample is a little below the true amplitude. — source: R2 (local run, labs/F1-72)
  18. F18 (l2-filter) Applied to the jumpy IMU of F1-65, this filter would shrink the 0.30 g vibration at 87 Hz to about 0.06 g. — source: R2 (local run, labs/F1-72)
  19. F19 (l2-filter) The price is delay: after a step, the output passes 63.2% in 10 samples, 10 ms. — source: R2 (local run, labs/F1-72)
  20. F20 (l2-filter) A filter cannot remove what aliasing has already folded into the pass band; that needs an analog anti-alias filter before the ADC (F1-64). — source: D3 O&S 3rd ed. ch. 5–7 (contents only)
  21. F21 (l2-ground) "Ground" is only a reference: the voltage every other voltage is measured against. A ground wire has resistance (and inductance), so a current through it makes a voltage along it. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. §8.1/§8.15/§8.16/§5.13–5.17 (TOC only)
  22. F22 (l2-ground) When a motor's return current flows through a piece of wire that is also part of a sensor's reference, the sensor's ground rises by I·R and the ADC sees the sensor voltage plus I·R: common-impedance coupling. — source: D2 Ott (not opened)
  23. F23 (l2-ground) In the forensic log, a 0.05 Ω shared segment and 3 A of motor current add 0.15 V to a 1.000 V sensor. — source: R3 (local run, labs/F1-72)
  24. F24 (l2-ground) Because it follows the motor current, the error is not random and averaging does nothing; the cure is to separate the return paths (a star ground, Figure 1), and in the model the error disappears completely. — source: R4 (local run, labs/F1-72)
  25. F25 (l2-ground) … : switching currents in loops create magnetic fields that induce voltages in nearby loops (reduce loop areas; twist signal and return together); fast voltage edges couple through stray capacitance (keep sensitive wires away from motor and PWM wires; shield). — source: D2 Ott (not opened)
  26. F26 (l2-ground) Supply noise spreads through shared supply wiring; decoupling capacitors close to each chip and separate supply paths for motors and logic (F1-71) keep it local. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. §8.1/§8.15/§8.16/§5.13–5.17 (TOC only)
  27. F27 (layer3) … r-order IIR filters (for example Butterworth) and FIR filters give steeper roll-off; the trade-off is always between attenuation, delay and phase distortion. Notch filters remove one known frequency (such as a motor's vibration) with little effect elsewhere. — source: D3 O&S 3rd ed. ch. 5–7 (contents only)
  28. F28 (layer3) Cut-off of the EMA. For small a, the −3 dB frequency is about a·fs/(2π) ≈ 16 Hz here; Listing 2's 17 Hz is close. The equivalent time constant is about 1/a samples. — source: D3 O&S 3rd ed. ch. 5–7 (contents only)
  29. F29 (layer3) … ysis. Logging a suspected source (motor current, PWM duty) alongside the disturbed signal and fitting a line (Listing in the key) turns "it seems worse when the motors run" into a measured coupling coefficient, here 0.0500 V/A — the shared resistance itself. — source: R4 (local run, labs/F1-72)
  30. F30 (layer3) Differential measurement. Measuring a sensor's output against its own ground pin (a differential ADC input) rejects most of the ground shift, up to the ADC's common-mode limits. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. §8.1/§8.15/§8.16/§5.13–5.17 (TOC only)
  31. F31 (layer3) Digital buses are not immune. I2C (F1-65) uses ground-referenced logic levels and open-drain lines with pull-ups; ground shifts and coupled edges cause bit errors and stuck buses, which a logic analyser shows directly. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. §8.1/§8.15/§8.16/§5.13–5.17 (TOC only)
  32. F32 (layer3) EMC rules. Products sold in many markets must meet electromagnetic compatibility regulations; the reasons are the same mechanisms, at larger scale. — source: D2 Ott (not opened)
  33. F33 (hardware) … h-current motor section from the sensitive analog section, routes the motor return directly to the battery connector, and ties the grounds at one point near the supply; a solid ground plane gives every signal a low-inductance return path directly beneath it. — source: D2 Ott (not opened)
  34. F34 (hardware) Each chip has decoupling capacitors next to its supply pins; motors often have small capacitors across their terminals to absorb brush noise (F1-69). — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. §8.1/§8.15/§8.16/§5.13–5.17 (TOC only)
  35. F35 (hardware) Sensor modules' datasheets give their supply-noise tolerance and recommended decoupling, and an ADC's datasheet its input type (single-ended or differential) and reference. — source: D5 LSM6DSOX DS12814 / RP2350 (partly opened)
  36. F36 (breaks) A backed-up drain is slow; ground shifts follow current in microseconds. PWM edges (F1-70) produce ground shifts at the switching frequency and above, where wire inductance matters as much as resistance. — source: D2 Ott (not opened)
  37. F37 (breaks) Water needs pipes; electric and magnetic fields do not. Coupling through the air (capacitive and inductive) has no plumbing equivalent. — source: D2 Ott (not opened)
  38. F38 (breaks) Kenji's readings wobble for many reasons; Listing 1's noise is ideal. It is Gaussian and independent; real noise often is not. — source: R1 (local run, labs/F1-72)
  39. F39 (transition) … and aliasing; the DFT that found the jumpy IMU's vibration; motor and supply currents. What changes: the two halves meet: power currents disturb sensor readings, and the task is to tell random noise (filter it) from interference (wire it out), with evidence. — source: D2 Ott (not opened)
  40. F40 (lab) Safety. Simulator: none. Optional hardware: motors clamped, no propellers, battery rules of F1-71, supervised for L2. — source: S1 guide (local)
  41. F41 (project) … ed tolerances. On hardware (untested in this build), the curriculum milestone H2's acceptance tests, verbatim: "Sensor readings are correct against a known value; a logic analyser trace of the I2C bus matches the specification's timing for the chosen speed." — source: C1 curriculum (map)
  42. F42 (forensic-key) … d 1.0 (about 3 A): about 1.15 V. The step is about 0.075 V per 1.5 A = 0.05 V/A: a resistance of 0.05 Ω. ground_check fits the whole log: slope 0.0500 V/A, correlation 0.999; with the shared segment removed (star ground), slope 0.0000 and correlation −0.023. — source: R4 (local run, labs/F1-72)
