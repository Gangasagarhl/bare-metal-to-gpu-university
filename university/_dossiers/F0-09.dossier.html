Notes per document (paraphrased): what each document was used for is given in the chapter's Sources list, in the same words as "Used for".

  • D1: packets can be dropped, reordered or duplicated; IP does not remove duplicates or guarantee order; TCP gives a reliable byte stream using sequence numbers, reordering, discarding duplicates and retransmitting.
  • D2: messages may be lost, duplicated or reordered; a node that gets no reply does not know whether its request arrived, and a node that is only slow can look as if it has crashed.
  • D3: struct, std::vector, std::map (iterates in order of its keys), std::string::substr and at() with its range check in Listing 1.
  • D4: IP carries blocks of data (datagrams) between computers named by addresses in the header (source and destination address); gateways (routers) forward datagrams between networks; IP has no acknowledgments and is not a reliable service.
  • D5: TCP segments are sent as internet datagrams; every octet has a sequence number; TCP detects losses and repairs them by retransmission after a timeout; segments may be lost because of errors or congestion; duplicates may arrive; TCP gives applications a reliable, in-order byte stream.
  • D6: the reliability goal in its classic wording: recover from data that is damaged, lost, duplicated, or delivered out of order, using sequence numbers, acknowledgments and retransmission. (RFC 793 is obsolete; D5 is the current standard.)
  • D7: from a computer's point of view the network is another input/output device; data flows across the network to another machine.
