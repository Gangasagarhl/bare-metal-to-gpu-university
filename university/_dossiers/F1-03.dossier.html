C. Facts (keyed to the chapter's claim tags)

  1. F1 [D3 §2.3.4] Table 4: coulomb = A s; volt = W/A; ohm = V/A; farad = C/V; joule; watt = J/s. Derived here: 1 A = 1 C/s, 1 V = 1 J/C, 1 W = 1 J/s, Ω × F = s. Used in: jargon, l2.
  2. F2 [D4] Pending: values of the kit's parts and meter (no document yet). Used in: jargon, layer1, layer3, transition.
  3. F3 [D5 §10.1] A battery separates charge by a chemical reaction; emf; terminal voltage V = ε − Ir is less than the emf when current flows; internal resistance grows as the battery runs down; chemistry sets the emf; a shorted battery heats up. Used in: jargon.
  4. F4 [D5 §10.3] Kirchhoff's junction rule (charge conservation at a node) and loop rule (the voltage changes around a loop add to zero). Used in: l2.
  5. F5 [D5 §7.2] Potential difference is the change in potential energy per unit charge; units J/C = volt; the zero of potential is an arbitrary choice; a battery's voltage is the potential difference between its terminals. Used in: jargon.
  6. F6 [D5 §9.2] Electrons collide with the atoms of the conductor and with each other; collisions produce thermal energy; drift velocity is small but the signal travels at nearly the speed of light, so a lamp lights at once. Used in: hardware.
  7. F7 [D5 §9.4] Ohm's law V = IR for ohmic components, R = V/I is the slope of the V–I line; Ohm's law is empirical, not universal; a diode is nonohmic, conducts in one direction, passes essentially no reverse current until breakdown. Used in: breaks, transition.
  8. F8 [D5 §9.5] P = IV for any device; P = I²R = V²/R for a resistor, dissipated as heat; E = Pt; a fuse wire heats and breaks above its rated current. Used in: jargon, layer1, l2, breaks, transition.
  9.
