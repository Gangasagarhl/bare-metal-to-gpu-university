C. Facts (keyed to the chapter's claim tags)

  1. F1 [D4 §3.8] Speculative and dynamically scheduled cores keep SC by checking load reordering (watching for evictions and incoming GetM requests, or replaying loads at commit); non-binding prefetches do not affect the model; "any feature or optimization is legal as long as it does not produce an end result (values returned by loads) that violates SC"; SC "does NOT dictate the order of coherence activity". Used in: layer 3 (why hardware relaxes; coherence still holds), hardware.
  2. F2 [D4 §1.1, §3.2–3.3] A memory consistency model specifies the allowed behaviours of multithreaded programs with shared memory; consistency is distinct from coherence. Used in: jargon (memory consistency model), transition.
  3. F3 [D6 6.10.2.2] Happens-before: sequenced-before or synchronizes-with, transitively. A data race is two potentially concurrent conflicting actions, at least one non-atomic, neither happening before the other; "Any such data race results in undefined behavior". Note: programs that prevent all data races with mutexes and memory_order::seq_cst operations "behave as if the operations executed by their constituent threads were simply interleaved" (SC for data-race-free programs). Used in: jargon (acquire and release), l2-cpp (acquire/release bullet, SC-DRF), breaks 3, transition, answers 6.
  4. F4 [D4 ch. 4, §4.4, §4.4.2, ch. 5, §5.4, §11.2.1 (titles only)] Store buffer, TSO as the relaxation of store→load order, fences and atomic RMW draining the buffer, weaker models (RVWMO, Power, Arm), SC for DRF, litmus tests: the chapter titles exist in the table of contents; the text could not be fetched. These statements are confirmed only at title level. Used in: jargon (store buffer, TSO, litmus test, fence), layer 1, l2-tso, l2-fence, layer 3, hardware, breaks 1–2, Figure 1 and 2 captions.
  5. F5 [D6 [atomics.order] (not opened)] memory_order values; release/acquire synchronisation; the single total order of seq_cst operations; seq_cst as the default; atomic_thread_fence. Not read in this pass; the chapter's bullets state the standard's rules from the author's knowledge. Used in: l2-cpp bullets, l2-fence.
  6. F6 [D7 (not opened)] xchg with a memory operand is a locked instruction that orders earlier and later loads and stores; the x86-64 ordering rules are TSO-like. Not opened; kept in the unverified box. Used in: jargon (fence), layer 3 (what the compiler emitted), unverified box.
  7. F7 [R1–R4] litmus_model: SC 20 orderings, no 0/0; store-buffer 80 orderings, 0/0 in 18. store_buffer.cc: 0/0 724 (relaxed), 916 (rel/acq), 0 (seq_cst) of 200,000 (0.36 % and 0.46 %). peterson: 718 lost with rel/acq, 0 with seq_cst. objdump: mov for release stores, xchg for seq_cst stores, plain mov loads. Used in: l2-sc, l2-tso, layer 3, worked example, code walk-through, forensic lab and key.
