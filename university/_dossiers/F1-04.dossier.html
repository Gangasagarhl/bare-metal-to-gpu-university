C. Facts (keyed to the chapter's claim tags)

  1. F1 [D5 §10.2] Series: the same current through each resistor, Req = sum; parallel: the same voltage across each, 1/Req = sum of 1/R. Used in: jargon, layer1, l2, breaks.
  2. F2 [D5 §10.3] Kirchhoff's junction rule (charge conservation at a node) and loop rule (the voltage changes around a loop add to zero). Used in: jargon, transition.
  3. F3 [D5 §9.3] R = ρL/A (longer and thinner resistors more); resistance varies with temperature (a filament's resistance changes by more than a factor of 10); resistor tolerance (e.g. ±10 %); carbon and metal-film resistors on a ceramic core. Used in: hardware.
  4. F4 [D5 §9.4] Ohm's law V = IR for ohmic components, R = V/I is the slope of the V–I line; Ohm's law is empirical, not universal; a diode is nonohmic, conducts in one direction, passes essentially no reverse current until breakdown. Used in: hardware, transition.
  5. F5 [D5 §9.5] P = IV for any device; P = I²R = V²/R for a resistor, dissipated as heat; E = Pt; a fuse wire heats and breaks above its rated current. Used in: l2.
  6. F6 [D6 §14.7] Poiseuille's law: laminar flow is proportional to the pressure difference; turbulence greatly increases the resistance to flow (p2 − p1 = RQ). Used in: breaks.
  7. F7 [D8 §3.4] Node voltage analysis writes KCL equations at the nodes; voltage division V = Vin × R2 ÷ (R1 + R2) used in worked examples. Used in: jargon, l2, layer3.
  8. F8 [D8 §3.5] Thévenin equivalence: a resistor-and-source network is equivalent at its terminals to an ideal source in series with a resistance; Vt = Voc (found by voltage division in the example), Rt = Voc ÷ Isc (the example's Rt equals the two divider resistors in parallel); a battery's internal resistance lowers its voltage under load. Used in: l2, layer3.
  9. F9 [R1] Outputs of this chapter's lab runs (R1 row in section B; toolchain in section E). Used in: worked, code.
  10. F10 [S1] Guide 'Honesty first' safety statement (local file). Used in: lab.
