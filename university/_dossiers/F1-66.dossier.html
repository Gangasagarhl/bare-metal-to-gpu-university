Fact list (keyed to the chapter's claim tags, in chapter order)

  1. F1 (jargon) Simple words: a sensor that gives a pulse for every small step a shaft turns. Analogy: the click of the pasta crank at every notch. Precise: a rotary sensor that outputs pulses as the shaft moves; position is known only relative to where counting started. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  2. F2 (jargon) … ection. Analogy: the second spring set a little behind the first. Precise: two square waves A and B, 90 electrical degrees out of phase; each cycle passes through four states (00, 01, 11, 10 in one direction), and the order of the states gives the direction. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  3. F3 (jargon) … crank. Precise: an encoder with L lines (pulses per channel per revolution) gives 4L counts per revolution when every edge of both channels is counted (x4 decoding), 2L or L with x2 or x1 decoding. Whether a datasheet quotes lines or counts must be checked. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  4. F4 (jargon) Simple words: one extra pulse once per turn, to find a known position. Analogy: a painted mark on the crank wheel. Precise: a third channel (often called Z or I) that pulses once per revolution and is used to reset or check the count at a reference angle. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  5. F5 (jargon) … oment, even just after power-on. Analogy: a crank wheel with the angle printed at every notch. Precise: a rotary sensor that outputs a code for the current angle; optical ones often use a Gray code so that only one bit changes between neighbouring positions. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  6. F6 (jargon) … mbering in which the next number always differs in exactly one bit. Analogy: a row of switches where you only ever flip one at a time to count. Precise: a binary code in which successive values differ in one bit; binary b converts to Gray g = b XOR (b >> 1). — source: D4 Harris & Harris (not opened)
  7. F7 (jargon) … u went by counting wheel turns. Analogy: Ravi measuring dough by counting clicks. Precise: estimation of displacement from wheel or joint rotation measurements; for a wheel of radius r, distance = 2πr × counts / CPR, valid only while the wheel does not slip. — source: D3 Lynch & Park (not opened)
  8. F8 (fig1) Figure 1. Quadrature signals. The forward order of states 00 → 01 → 11 → 10 matches the decoder table of Listing 1 (this course's convention; which channel leads in which direction is defined by the encoder's datasheet, D2). — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  9. F9 (fig2) Figure 2. The decoder of Listing 1 as a finite state machine (F1-19). Clockwise steps count +1, anticlockwise −1; diagonal jumps mean at least two edges happened between samples. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  10. F10 (l2-how) … produces a pulse per small step. Optical encoders shine light through a slotted disk onto photodetectors; magnetic encoders sense a magnetised ring with Hall-effect or magnetoresistive sensors, which is how the kit's encoder works. Both produce square waves. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  11. F11 (l2-how) … y voltage come from the named encoder's datasheet; for the kit's Pololu gearmotor the page gives 12 counts per motor-shaft revolution counting both edges of both channels, a 2.7–18 V encoder supply, and outputs that are driven low or pulled up through 10 kΩ. — source: D2 Pololu 5190 product page
  12. F12 (l2-quad) … nnels 90° apart (quadrature), each cycle has four states and four edges. If you sample A and B and compare each new state with the previous one, there are 16 combinations: four "no change", four "+1", four "−1" and four "both changed" (Figure 2's diagonals). — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  13. F13 (l2-quad) … < 2) | current. Fed the input 00 01 11 10 00 01 11 10 00 it counts to 8; fed the reverse order it counts down; and the jump 01 → 10 is reported as ERROR: both channels changed, so the decoder cannot know whether two steps went forwards or two went backwards. — source: R1 (local run, labs/F1-66)
  14. F14 (l2-quad) Counting every edge of both channels is x4 decoding: an encoder with L lines gives 4L counts per revolution; the kit encoder's "12 CPR" is already this x4 count (3 lines per channel), and the order of the A-B transitions gives the direction. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  15. F15 (l2-convert) Angle = 360° × count / CPR. On a wheel of radius r, distance = 2πr × count / CPR: this is odometry, and it is only as good as the assumption that the wheel does not slip. — source: D3 Lynch & Park (not opened)
  16. F16 (l2-convert) Speed can be estimated in two ways, which Listing 2 compares with a pretend 400 CPR encoder: — source: R2 (local run, labs/F1-66)
  17. F17 (l2-convert) … (the frequency method): count edges in a fixed time T; speed = Δcount / (CPR × T). Its resolution is one count per window, 1/(400 × 0.010 s) = 0.25 rev/s here, so at 0.1 rev/s the three windows read 0.25, 0, 0: useless at low speed, excellent at high speed. — source: R2 (local run, labs/F1-66)
  18. F18 (l2-convert) … ges (the period method): a timer measures the time between two edges; speed = 1 / (CPR × Δt). With a pretend 1 MHz timer it reads 0.1000 rev/s exactly at 0.1 rev/s, but at 150 rev/s only 16 ticks separate the edges and the estimate is 156.25 rev/s, 4 % high. — source: R2 (local run, labs/F1-66)
  19. F19 (l2-convert) Practical speed estimators switch between the two or combine them. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  20. F20 (l2-abs) … it moved since counting began; after power-on, a robot arm with incremental encoders must find a reference (an index pulse or a limit switch). An absolute encoder outputs the angle itself, as a code read in parallel or over a serial bus such as SPI (F1-47). — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  21. F21 (l2-abs) … code, in which neighbouring positions differ in one bit: if the disk stops on a boundary, a misread bit gives one of the two neighbouring positions, never a far-away one. With plain binary, 011 → 100 changes three bits at once, and a misread could give 111. — source: D4 Harris & Harris (not opened)
  22. F22 (layer3) … per edge: with x4 decoding, the edge rate is 4 × lines × rev/s. If two edges happen between samples, the decoder sees a diagonal (invalid); if three happen, the states look like one step backwards; if four, like no movement. The forensic lab shows all three. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  23. F23 (layer3) … is missed regardless of what the software is doing; otherwise, edge interrupts on both channels (F1-42) must be fast enough for the highest edge rate. Whether the kit's microcontroller has such a timer mode is in its reference manual (unverified box below). — source: D5 kit MCU datasheet (not opened)
  24. F24 (layer3) … Long cables and motor switching put spikes on encoder lines (F1-72). Differential outputs, digital glitch filters on the inputs and the "invalid transition" counter are the usual defences; a non-zero invalid count in normal running means something is wrong. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  25. F25 (layer3) … nter width. A 16-bit hardware counter wraps after 65,536 counts; compute differences with unsigned arithmetic and cast the difference to a signed 16-bit value, which is correct as long as fewer than 32,768 counts pass between reads (two's complement, MA102). — source: D4 Harris & Harris (not opened)
  26. F26 (layer3) … sh but has fewer counts. The ratio and backlash come from the gearmotor's datasheet: the kit's gearmotor has a 100.37:1 gearbox, so one output-shaft revolution is 12 × 100.37 ≈ 1204.4 counts (arithmetic from the page's numbers; backlash is not stated there). — source: D2 Pololu 5190 product page
  27. F27 (hardware) An optical encoder's photodetectors produce small analog currents; a comparator inside the encoder squares them into logic levels, sometimes with an interpolator that creates more edges per slot. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  28. F28 (hardware) … the microcontroller side the A and B lines enter digital input pins; in a timer's encoder mode, the timer's counter register is incremented or decremented by a small hardware state machine like Figure 2 on every edge, and the software only reads the counter. — source: D5 kit MCU datasheet (not opened)
  29. F29 (breaks) Ravi hears every click; a polling program may not. A person listening hears each click when it happens. A program that looks at the lines every 0.5 ms only sees the state at those moments; edges in between are lost or misread. — source: R3 (local run, labs/F1-66)
  30. F30 (breaks) The two springs click; the encoder's channels have levels. Decoding uses the levels of both channels at each moment, not just "a click happened", which is why the table needs the previous and current state of both. — source: R1 (local run, labs/F1-66)
  31. F31 (breaks) Dough does not slip on the crank; wheels slip on floors. The count measures the wheel's rotation exactly, but the distance the robot moved only while the wheel grips. — source: D3 Lynch & Park (not opened)
  32. F32 (transition) … ith a gyroscope; F1-19 built finite state machines; F1-64 introduced sampling and aliasing. What carries over: sampling rules (here: at least one sample per edge), two's-complement arithmetic, register reads, and the habit of logging the raw signal to debug. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  33. F33 (transition) What changes: the signal is already digital, so there is no ADC; position comes from counting, not integrating, so there is no drift while counts are not lost; but one missed edge is a permanent error, and the decoder must keep up with the fastest edge rate. — source: D1 AoE 3rd ed. (no encoder section; unchecked)
  34. F34 (lab) … nd the wheel or shaft is free of fingers, hair and cables before power is applied; an adult supervises L2 learners; low voltage from the kit only; no propellers. Turn the shaft by hand first with the driver unpowered. Disconnect power before changing wiring. — source: S1 guide (local)
