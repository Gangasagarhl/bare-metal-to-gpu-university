C. Facts (keyed to the chapter's claim tags)

  1. F1 [D4] Pending: values of the kit's parts and meter (no document yet). Used in: jargon, layer1, l2, lab.
  2. F2 [D5 §10.1] A battery separates charge by a chemical reaction; emf; terminal voltage V = ε − Ir is less than the emf when current flows; internal resistance grows as the battery runs down; chemistry sets the emf; a shorted battery heats up. Used in: l2.
  3. F3 [D5 §10.4] Voltmeter in parallel, must have a very large resistance; ammeter in series, very low resistance; meters alter the circuit; an ohmmeter needs the component isolated; digital meters use an analog-to-digital converter. Used in: jargon, layer1, l2, layer3, breaks, transition.
  4. F4 [D5 §9.4] Ohm's law V = IR for ohmic components, R = V/I is the slope of the V–I line; Ohm's law is empirical, not universal; a diode is nonohmic, conducts in one direction, passes essentially no reverse current until breakdown. Used in: hardware.
  5. F5 [D5 §9.5] P = IV for any device; P = I²R = V²/R for a resistor, dissipated as heat; E = Pt; a fuse wire heats and breaks above its rated current. Used in: hardware.
  6. F6 [D13] An ammeter's mA input is a shunt in series; its burden voltage is subtracted from the circuit and reduces the current measured, most in low-voltage circuits; shunt and protection fuse together add resistance. Used in: jargon, layer1, l2, hardware.
  7. F7 [D21] A DC voltage reading changes sign when the leads are swapped; current is measured in series, and a meter in current mode across a source can be damaged or blow its fuse; resistance is measured with power off; a voltmeter has a finite internal resistance. Used in: jargon, layer1, l2, breaks.
