C. Fact list

  • F1 A short circuit is a low-resistance path between the terminals of a voltage source; its heat can melt or ignite nearby materials. Source: D4 §20.6. Used in: Jargon (Short circuit); Layer 2 table; hardware; Breaks 1.
  • F2 Shock hazards depend on current; 120 V AC household electricity is the book's example; wet skin lowers resistance. Source: D4 §20.6. Used in: Jargon (Mains); Layer 2 table (drinks).
  • F3 Power turned into heat in a resistance is P = I²R; by Ohm's law a near-zero resistance gives a large current. Source: D5 §9.4, §9.5. Used in: hardware.
  • F4 A LiPo maker warns that damaged cells can lead to fire, that cells lack overcharge/deep-discharge protection, to avoid short circuits and never to leave charging unattended. Source: D6. Used in: Jargon (LiPo battery).
  • F5 The WHO Surgical Safety Checklist exists for surgical teams. Source: D7. Used in: Layer 1 (checklists).
  • F6 Supervision, no mains, no LiPo, no propellers, simulator first. Source: S1. Used in: Layers 1–2; Figure 1; safety boxes.
  • F7 The reference kit uses AA cells, never LiPo. Source: K1. Used in: unverified box; D2.
  • F8 Checklist program outputs (gate opens; incident run). Source: R1. Used in: Worked example; forensic.
