C. Facts (keyed to the chapter's claim tags)

  1. F1 [D3 Table 7] SI prefixes: k = 10^3, M = 10^6, m = 10^−3, µ = 10^−6, n = 10^−9, p = 10^−12. Used in: jargon.
  2. F2 [D3 §2.3.4] Table 4: coulomb = A s; volt = W/A; ohm = V/A; farad = C/V; joule; watt = J/s. Derived here: 1 A = 1 C/s, 1 V = 1 J/C, 1 W = 1 J/s, Ω × F = s. Used in: jargon, l2.
  3. F3 [D4] Pending: values of the kit's parts and meter (no document yet). Used in: jargon.
  4. F4 [D5 §10.5] RC charging q(t) = Cε(1 − e^−t/RC); τ = RC in seconds; 0.632 of the final value after one τ; discharge leaves 0.368 after one τ; initial current ε/R. Used in: jargon, layer1, l2, breaks, transition.
  5. F5 [D5 §8.1] A capacitor is two conductors separated by an insulator (dielectric); Q = C V; 1 F = 1 C/V, a very large unit; practical values from pF upward; larger plate area and smaller separation give more capacitance; foil-and-insulator construction, sometimes rolled; electrolytic capacitors work only with the correct polarity. Used in: jargon, layer1, l2, hardware, breaks.
  6. F6 [D5 §9.1] Current is the rate of flow of charge; 1 A = 1 C/s; a complete path is needed; conventional current is the direction positive charge would move, from + to − outside the source; in metal wires electrons carry the current, opposite to conventional current. Used in: l2.
  7. F7 [D10] Example capacitor guideline: electrolytic capacitors have polarity; reverse voltage can cause venting or rupture; do not exceed the rated voltage; leakage current; standard tolerance ±20 %; capacitance can fall after overheating. Used in: jargon, layer3, transition, lab.
  8. F8 [D20] Charging and discharging internal and load capacitance causes dynamic power, proportional to the switching frequency (P = Cpd × VCC² × fI × NSW). Used in: hardware.
  9. F9 [R1] Outputs of this chapter's lab runs (R1 row in section B; toolchain in section E). Used in: jargon, l2, worked, code.
  10. F10 [S1] Guide 'Honesty first' safety statement (local file). Used in: lab.
