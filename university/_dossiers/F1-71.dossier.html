Notes and quotes (paraphrased unless quoted)

  • KIT.md LiPo rules (local): follow the maker's and charger maker's instructions; charge only with a balance charger in LiPo mode, at or below the rated current, in a LiPo bag or metal box, on a non-flammable surface, never unattended; never use a puffed, dented, punctured or unexpectedly warm pack; store at storage voltage; kids' kits use AA cells only. The chapter's safety box now cites these.
  • The pack maker's datasheet is not named in KIT.md and no charger manual was opened; the chapter keeps every limit as "from the maker".
  • All numbers in the chapter's programs (3.6 V / 2.0 Ah cell, OCV table, 0.05 Ω, 3.20 V cutoff, 10.8 V pack, 0.15 Ω / 0.10 Ω, 7.0 V threshold, 100 ms boot) are pretend exercise values (ruling A4).

Fact list (keyed to the chapter's claim tags, in chapter order)

  1. F1 (jargon) … nalogy: stacking tanks on top of each other raises the pressure; putting them side by side holds more water. Precise: in an nSmP pack, n cells in series multiply the voltage by n, and m strings in parallel multiply the capacity by m; energy scales with both. — source: D4 Linden (not opened)
  2. F2 (jargon) … much those litres can do at the tank's pressure. Precise: capacity in ampere-hours is the charge delivered under stated test conditions; energy in watt-hours is approximately capacity × average voltage. Both are rated by the maker under specific conditions. — source: D4 Linden (not opened)
  3. F3 (jargon) … ith the battery's size. Analogy: "1C" empties the tank in about an hour; "2C" in about half an hour. Precise: current divided by the rated capacity in Ah (per hour); the maker states maximum continuous and burst discharge C-rates and the maximum charge rate. — source: D1 battery maker (KIT.md record only)
  4. F4 (jargon) … nalogy: the narrow tank outlet: the faster the flow, the more pressure is lost there. Precise: to first order, terminal voltage = open-circuit voltage − I·Rint; Rint varies with temperature, state of charge and age, and the drop also heats the cell (I²Rint). — source: D4 Linden (not opened)
  5. F5 (jargon) … which the pump must stop. Precise: state of charge (SoC) is the remaining fraction of capacity; the open-circuit voltage depends on it in a chemistry-specific way; the discharge cutoff is the minimum voltage the maker allows, below which the cell is damaged. — source: D1 battery maker (KIT.md record only)
  6. F6 (jargon) … p, machine and toilet in the restaurant with how much water each uses per hour. Precise: a table of loads with their average and peak currents (or powers) at each supply rail, summed to size the battery, regulators, wiring and fuses, with an explicit margin. — source: D3 AoE 3rd ed. ch. 9 / §8.15 (TOC only)
  7. F7 (jargon) … achine shutting down when the pressure drops. Precise: a supply voltage below a device's minimum operating level; microcontrollers usually include a brown-out detector that holds or puts the chip in reset below a configured threshold, given in its datasheet. — source: D5 kit MCU datasheet (not opened)
  8. F8 (jargon) Simple words: starting a motor gently instead of all at once. Analogy: Hamid opening the dishwasher's valve slowly. Precise: ramping a motor's command (or a supply's output) over a set time to limit the inrush current and the resulting voltage drop. — source: R4 (local run, labs/F1-71)
  9. F9 (lipo) Lithium-polymer (LiPo) and other lithium cells store a lot of energy; a damaged, overcharged, over-discharged or short-circuited cell can overheat, vent and catch fire. — source: D1 battery maker (KIT.md record only)
  10. F10 (lipo) Course rules: — source: S1 guide + KIT.md safety notes (local)
  11. F11 (fig1) Figure 1. The first-order battery model used in Listings 2 and 3 (D4), and series/parallel pack layouts (Listing 1). Real cells add slower effects (polarisation, temperature) that this model leaves out. — source: D4 Linden (not opened)
  12. F12 (fig2) Figure 2. The forensic robot's wiring in the power model (R3): the MCU shares the 0.10 Ω wire with the motors. The dashed line is one of the fixes tried in the key. Values are pretend. — source: R3 (local run, labs/F1-71)
  13. F13 (fig3) Figure 3. Points from the detailed trace of the forensic log (R3). The voltage falls because the start-up current flows through the pack's internal resistance and the shared wire. — source: R3 (local run, labs/F1-71)
  14. F14 (l2-pack) Listing 1 uses a pretend cell (3.6 V nominal, 2.0 Ah — not the numbers of any real chemistry). In series, voltages add: 3S gives 10.8 V. In parallel, capacities add: 3S2P gives 10.8 V, 4.0 Ah and 43.2 Wh. — source: R1 (local run, labs/F1-71)
  15. F15 (l2-pack) The C-rate expresses current relative to capacity: for the 4.0 Ah pack, 1C = 4 A and 5C = 20 A. — source: D4 Linden (not opened)
  16. F16 (l2-pack) A robot drawing 2.5 A on average would ideally run 1.6 h; using only 80% of the capacity (our own design margin, not a battery rule) gives 77 minutes. — source: R1 (local run, labs/F1-71)
  17. F17 (l2-pack) The maker's datasheet states the capacity, the nominal voltage, the maximum continuous and burst discharge currents, the charge limits and the safe voltage window. — source: D1 battery maker (KIT.md record only)
  18. F18 (l2-sag) A real cell is not an ideal voltage source. To first order it is an open-circuit voltage (OCV), which falls as the charge is used, in series with an internal resistance (Figure 1). — source: D4 Linden (not opened)
  19. F19 (l2-sag) … cutoff 3.20 V) at four currents. At 1 A the terminal starts at 3.95 V and the cell gives 1.83 Ah over 110 min; at 10 A it starts at 3.50 V and reaches the cutoff after 4.0 min having given only 0.66 Ah; at 20 A it starts at 3.00 V, already below the cutoff. — source: R2 (local run, labs/F1-71)
  20. F20 (l2-sag) The charge did not vanish: the I·R drop made the terminal voltage reach the cutoff while charge was still inside. Heat in the cell grows as I²R: 0.05 W at 1 A, 5 W at 10 A. — source: R2 (local run, labs/F1-71)
  21. F21 (l2-sag) Real cells show more effects (temperature, ageing, slower recovery), and their usable capacity at high current is in the maker's discharge curves. — source: D1 battery maker (KIT.md record only)
  22. F22 (l2-budget) … ly rail, average current and peak current: motors (running, starting and stalled, F1-69), the microcontroller, sensors, radio, camera. Sum the averages for run time and the peaks for wiring, fuses, regulators and the battery's maximum current, with a margin. — source: D3 AoE 3rd ed. ch. 9 / §8.15 (TOC only)
  23. F23 (l2-budget) A regulator that steps the battery down for the electronics needs its input to stay above its own minimum (dropout) under the worst sag; that minimum is in its datasheet. — source: D3 AoE 3rd ed. ch. 9 / §8.15 (TOC only)
  24. F24 (l2-brown) … tance and every shared wire, and the voltage at every other load drops by I × Rshared. If the microcontroller's input falls below its minimum, its brown-out detector resets it; the motors stop, the voltage recovers, the MCU boots and starts the motors again. — source: D5 kit MCU datasheet (not opened)
  25. F25 (l2-brown) In the forensic model (Figure 2) this loop repeats every 102 ms: 11 resets in 1.2 s. — source: R3 (local run, labs/F1-71)
  26. F26 (l2-brown) Two independent fixes each remove all resets in the model: feeding the MCU from the pack terminals (so it no longer shares the 0.10 Ω wire), or a 300 ms soft start of the motors. — source: R4 (local run, labs/F1-71)
  27. F27 (layer3) … battery models. Adding one or more RC pairs to the OCV-plus-R model captures the slower voltage recovery after a load step; parameters are fitted from pulse tests. State-of-charge estimation combines current counting (coulomb counting) with OCV corrections. — source: D4 Linden (not opened)
  28. F28 (layer3) … ulti-cell lithium packs balance each cell through a separate connector, and a battery management system (BMS) or protection circuit disconnects on over- or under-voltage, over-current or temperature. Which protection a given pack has is in its documentation. — source: D1 battery maker (KIT.md record only)
  29. F29 (layer3) Distribution. Star distribution from the pack, separate wires for motors and logic, a bulk capacitor at the driver and local decoupling at each chip reduce the voltage that one load's current imposes on another (F1-72). — source: D3 AoE 3rd ed. ch. 9 / §8.15 (TOC only)
  30. F30 (layer3) Regulators. A linear regulator wastes (Vin − Vout) × I as heat; a switching regulator is more efficient but adds ripple and noise. Both have a minimum input voltage. — source: D3 AoE 3rd ed. ch. 9 / §8.15 (TOC only)
  31. F31 (layer3) Brown-out detector settings. The threshold should be set so the chip resets cleanly before its voltage is too low to run correctly; a chip that runs on below its minimum can corrupt memory or flash writes. — source: D5 kit MCU datasheet (not opened)
  32. F32 (hardware) … ile electrons travel through the external circuit; the cell voltage depends on the electrode chemistry and the state of charge. A pouch ("LiPo") cell has a soft case that swells when the cell is damaged or abused, which is why a puffy pack is a warning sign. — source: D4 Linden (not opened)
  33. F33 (hardware) On the electronics side, a microcontroller's supply supervisor compares its supply against a reference and holds the core in reset while the supply is below the threshold; the threshold options and the minimum operating voltage are in the MCU datasheet. — source: D5 kit MCU datasheet (not opened)
  34. F34 (breaks) A water tank's pressure depends on height; a cell's voltage depends on chemistry and state of charge. The OCV curve is not linear and differs between chemistries. — source: D4 Linden (not opened)
  35. F35 (breaks) A narrow outlet only limits flow; internal resistance also heats the cell. High current warms a battery from inside, which matters for safety. — source: R2 (local run, labs/F1-71)
  36. F36 (breaks) Water does not catch fire. A damaged or abused lithium cell can. No part of the analogy covers that: the safety box does. — source: D1 battery maker (KIT.md record only)
  37. F37 (transition) … d its start-up current; Ohm's law (HW101) on every wire. What changes: the supply now has resistance and a limited store of energy, so one load's current changes every other load's voltage, and the battery itself becomes a safety hazard that must be managed. — source: D3 AoE 3rd ed. ch. 9 / §8.15 (TOC only)
  38. F38 (lab) Safety. Read the LiPo safety box before touching any battery. Supervised for L2. Any physical step follows the battery maker's instructions; no step here overrides them. — source: S1 guide + KIT.md safety notes (local)
