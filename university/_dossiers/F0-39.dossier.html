C. Fact list

  • F1 Electric current is the rate at which charge flows; the unit is the ampere (one coulomb per second). Source: D3 §9.1. Used in: Jargon (Electricity, Current); Layer 2.
  • F2 In metal wires current is carried by electrons; conventional current is drawn in the direction positive charge would flow, from + to −. Source: D3 §9.1. Used in: Jargon; Layer 3; Figure 1.
  • F3 A complete path (circuit) from the positive to the negative terminal is needed for current; a switch opens or closes that path. Source: D3 §9.1. Used in: Jargon (Circuit); Layer 1; Breaks 1–2; Transition.
  • F4 Ohm's law V = IR; resistance is voltage divided by current, in ohms; a resistor is ohmic. Source: D3 §9.4. Used in: Jargon (Voltage, Resistance, Ohm's law); Layer 2.
  • F5 Power gained or lost by a device is P = IV (watts); a resistor gives off its energy as heat. Source: D3 §9.5. Used in: Layer 3.
  • F6 A battery keeps a potential difference between its + and − terminals, using energy from chemical reactions; a battery can be made of several cells. Source: D3 §10.1. Used in: Jargon (Battery).
  • F7 An inductor is a coil of wire whose induced voltage opposes changes in current. Source: D3 §14.2. Used in: Breaks 3.
  • F8 Lights come on as soon as a switch is flicked because conductors are full of free charges that push the charges ahead of them; the signal travels much faster than the drift. Source: D4 §20.1. Used in: How the hardware actually does it.
  • F9 Electric shock and thermal hazards of household voltages. Source: D4 §20.6. Used in: Breaks (wall sockets).
  • F10 Listing 1 prints 20, 10, 10 and 0 mA for loops A–D. Source: R1. Used in: Code walk-through; forensic key.
