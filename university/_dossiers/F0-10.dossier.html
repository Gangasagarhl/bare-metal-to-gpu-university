Notes per document (paraphrased): what each document was used for is given in the chapter's Sources list, in the same words as "Used for".

  • D1: robots made of links and joints moved by actuators, typically electric motors; sensors that measure the robot's motion; the robot's control computer; feedback control to cope with uncertainty; robot simulation software as a teaching tool.
  • D2: a motor driver chip: its input pins (small logic signals) control the outputs that drive DC motors from a separate motor supply.
  • D3: a drone as an unmanned robotic vehicle, manually or autonomously controlled; the flight controller connects to the sensors and to the motors that stabilise and move the vehicle; gyroscope and accelerometer among the required sensors; motors driven through electronic speed controllers (ESCs); armed vehicles are dangerous because propellers can start spinning.
  • D4: robots are uncertain about the world because of sensor limitations and noise; probabilistic methods weigh and combine sensor evidence; odometry errors can grow large over time; sensor feedback is central.
