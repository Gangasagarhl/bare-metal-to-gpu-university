FactStatement (paraphrased; short quotes marked)SourceUsed in
F1A robot's links are moved by actuators, typically electrically driven (DC or AC motors, steppers, hydraulic or pneumatic cylinders); sensors such as encoders measure joint displacement; the robot controller turns the task into actuator commands.D2 Ch. 1Jargon (Robot, Sensor, Actuator), Layer 2
F2"This basic feedback loop of sensing, computation, and actuation is the central concept in control."D1 §1.3Jargon (Robot, Sense–think–act), Figure 1, Layer 2, Transition
F3A digital controller reads sensors through an A/D converter and drives actuators through a D/A converter; a system clock synchronises them.D1 §1.3Jargon (Sensor), How the hardware actually does it
F4Systems connected in a cycle form a closed loop; breaking the interconnection gives an open loop.D1 §1.1Layer 2
F5Nested loops are a common architecture; inner loops are tuned first. Layered architectures separate decision-making, path planning and path following.D1 §1.5, §1.8Layer 3 note
F6A motor is connected to a bi-directional motor driver (an H-bridge) wired between the power source and the motor; the computer's GPIO pins connect to the driver's inputs.D8 §15.1.6How the hardware actually does it
F7Robots are uncertain about their world; uncertainty arises from sensor limitations and noise.D3 §1Where the analogy breaks
F8A computer controller operates periodically: sensors are sampled and the main loop runs once every sampling period.D1 §11.5Where the analogy breaks
F9Input and output devices are connected to the computer (memory-mapped I/O).D5 §5.1.6Transition box
