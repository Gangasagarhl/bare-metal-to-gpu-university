Notes and quotes (paraphrased unless quoted)

  • SI Brochure §2.2: "the speed of light in vacuum, c, is 299 792 458 m/s"; §2.3.1 "The metre": "defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the speed of light in vacuum, c, to be 299 792 458".
  • Arithmetic check of R1: 2 m round trip / 299 792 458 m/s = 6.671 ns per metre of range, as the chapter states.
  • HZ 2nd ed. 2004 table of contents: chapter 6 camera models, chapter 9 epipolar geometry, chapter 12 structure computation; the rectified-stereo formula Z = f·B/d is the similar-triangle derivation shown in the chapter's Figure 2, not quoted from the book.
  • IEC webstore: IEC 60825-1:2014, edition 3.0, 2014-05-15; abstract describes classification of lasers and laser products by hazard. The chapter claims nothing about the standard's content beyond its title and purpose.

Fact list (keyed to the chapter's claim tags, in chapter order)

  1. F1 (jargon) … g distance by timing how long a signal takes to go out and come back. Analogy: Kwame's clap and its echo in the cellar. Precise: a ranging method in which range d = v·t/2, where t is the round-trip time and v the propagation speed; for light in vacuum v = c. — source: D1 Thrun et al. (not opened)
  2. F2 (jargon) … and timing each echo. Precise: light detection and ranging: an active sensor that emits light (usually laser pulses or modulated light) and measures range from the returned light; a scanning LiDAR sweeps the beam to produce many (angle, range) measurements. — source: D1 Thrun et al. (not opened)
  3. F3 (jargon) … from echo times in each direction. Precise: a scan is a set of (bearing, range) readings from one sweep; converting each to Cartesian coordinates (x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ in the sensor frame) gives a 2D point set, or a 3D point cloud for multi-beam sensors. — source: D1 Thrun et al. (not opened)
  4. F4 (jargon) … echo at all. Precise: beyond its maximum range, or when the surface returns too little light, a range sensor reports no valid measurement; drivers encode this as a special value (zero, the maximum, infinity, or a flag) defined in the sensor's documentation. — source: D1 Thrun et al. (not opened)
  5. F5 (jargon) … the crate in front of the back wall: the clap's echo comes from the crate, and the wall behind it is not heard. Precise: a range sensor measures only the first surface along each beam; regions behind it produce no measurements (a "shadow" in the point set). — source: D1 Thrun et al. (not opened)
  6. F6 (jargon) … een the left and right camera images. Analogy: the pepper grinder jumping when Ines switches eyes. Precise: for two rectified cameras with focal length f (pixels) and baseline B, a point at depth Z appears shifted by disparity d = f·B/Z pixels, so Z = f·B/d. — source: D3 HZ 2nd ed. ch. 6/9/12 (contents only)
  7. F7 (jargon) … th pattern on the counter and seeing the stripes shift over the bowls. Precise: active triangulation: a projector casts a known pattern and a camera at a known offset observes it; depth follows from the pattern's displacement, by the same geometry as stereo. — source: D3 HZ 2nd ed. ch. 6/9/12 (contents only)
  8. F8 (fig1) … (D1, D2). Right: a scan; each beam reports the first surface it meets, so the far wall behind the box has a shadow, as in the text map of Listing 2's output (R2). Not to scale; real scanners use many more beams (the number comes from the sensor's datasheet). — source: D1 Thrun et al. (not opened)
  9. F9 (fig2) Figure 2. Stereo triangulation for two parallel (rectified) cameras: disparity d = x_L − x_R in pixels, f in pixels, B in metres. Similar triangles give Z = f·B/d (D3); Listing 3 evaluates it. Not to scale. — source: D3 HZ 2nd ed. ch. 6/9/12 (contents only)
  10. F10 (l2-tof) Like Kwame's clap, a pulsed ToF sensor emits a short light pulse and measures the round-trip time t to the echo; the range is d = c·t/2. — source: D1 Thrun et al. (not opened)
  11. F11 (l2-tof) The speed of light in vacuum, c = 299,792,458 m/s, is exact by the definition of the metre; in air light is slightly slower, a correction that matters only at high precision. — source: D2 SI Brochure 9th ed. §2.2/§2.3.1
  12. F12 (l2-tof) The numbers are small: Listing 1 prints 6.671 ns for 1 m and 66.713 ns for 10 m. A timer that counts in ticks of Δt resolves range in steps of c·Δt/2: 149.90 mm for 1 ns ticks, 14.99 mm for 100 ps ticks. — source: R1 (local run, labs/F1-67)
  13. F13 (l2-tof) Pulsed sensors therefore need very fine timing electronics, or they average many pulses. — source: D5 AoE 3rd ed. §12.6 (TOC only)
  14. F14 (l2-phase) … modulate their light at a frequency fm and measure the phase shift φ of the returned light; the range is d = c·φ / (4π fm). Because phase repeats every 2π, ranges repeat every c / (2 fm): a target just beyond that distance looks very close (range ambiguity). — source: D5 AoE 3rd ed. §12.6 (TOC only)
  15. F15 (l2-phase) This follows from the definitions: a full cycle of phase is one modulation period, and the light travels out and back. The modulation frequencies and how a product resolves the ambiguity are in its datasheet. — source: D4 range-sensor datasheet (none in kit)
  16. F16 (l2-scan) A 2D scanning LiDAR rotates one beam (or a mirror) and reports ranges at regular bearings; one revolution is a scan. In the sensor's frame, a reading (θ, r) is the point (r cos θ, r sin θ). — source: D1 Thrun et al. (not opened)
  17. F17 (l2-scan) … 4 m room with a 0.6 m box, one beam per degree, ray casting to the nearest wall. Its text map shows the room's outline and the occlusion shadow on the right wall behind the box: those wall cells have no points because every beam aimed there ends on the box. — source: R2 (local run, labs/F1-67)
  18. F18 (l2-scan) A scan is not taken at one instant: if the robot moves during the sweep, points from the start and end of the sweep are in different robot positions (motion distortion), which software must correct. — source: D1 Thrun et al. (not opened)
  19. F19 (l2-errors) … of "Probabilistic Robotics" lists four ways a single range reading is produced: a correct hit with small noise; an unexpected nearer object (a person walking past); a maximum-range reading when nothing returned; and random readings from unexplained failures. — source: D1 Thrun et al. (not opened)
  20. F20 (l2-errors) Dark, shiny, transparent and very oblique surfaces return too little light or reflect it away; glass may be seen through; a beam that touches the edge of a near object and a far wall may report a "mixed pixel" between them. — source: D1 Thrun et al. (not opened)
  21. F21 (l2-stereo) Ines's two eyes are a stereo pair. For two parallel cameras with focal length f (in pixels) and baseline B, a point at depth Z has disparity d = f·B/Z, so Z = f·B/d. — source: D3 HZ 2nd ed. ch. 6/9/12 (contents only)
  22. F22 (l2-stereo) With a pretend f = 700 px and B = 0.12 m, Listing 3 gives 1.00 m at 84 px and 8.00 m at 10.5 px. — source: R3 (local run, labs/F1-67)
  23. F23 (l2-stereo) Because Z is inversely proportional to d, a fixed disparity error δ produces a depth error of about Z²·δ/(f·B): a half-pixel error spreads the estimate over 0.012 m at 1 m but 0.764 m at 8 m, 64 times more for 8 times the distance. — source: R3 (local run, labs/F1-67)
  24. F24 (l2-stereo) Stereo also needs texture: a blank wall gives nothing to match. Structured light fixes that by projecting its own pattern, and uses the same triangulation. — source: D3 HZ 2nd ed. ch. 6/9/12 (contents only)
  25. F25 (layer3) … The tick of Listing 1 limits resolution; accuracy also depends on the pulse shape, the detector's timing walk with signal strength, temperature and calibration. Datasheets specify accuracy over a stated range of target reflectivities; compare like with like. — source: D5 AoE 3rd ed. §12.6 (TOC only)
  26. F26 (layer3) … acing. Points on a surface at range r are spaced about r·Δθ apart; at 1° spacing (Listing 2) that is 17 mm at 1 m and 70 mm at 4 m, so thin objects (a chair leg) can fall between beams at long range. The beam itself also has a width that grows with distance. — source: D1 Thrun et al. (not opened)
  27. F27 (layer3) Measurement models. Probabilistic robotics treats each reading as a draw from a mixture of the four cases above, with parameters learned from data; the max-range case is a spike at rmax and must be handled explicitly, not as a real obstacle at rmax or at 0. — source: D1 Thrun et al. (not opened)
  28. F28 (layer3) Stereo matching and calibration. Z = f·B/d assumes rectified images (rows aligned) and calibrated f, B and principal points; matching chooses d per pixel by comparing small windows, and fails on repeated patterns and occluded regions. — source: D3 HZ 2nd ed. ch. 6/9/12 (contents only)
  29. F29 (layer3) Interference. Two active sensors of the same kind can see each other's light; products use coding, timing or modulation schemes to reduce this, documented per product. — source: D4 range-sensor datasheet (none in kit)
  30. F30 (layer3) Data volume. A 3D LiDAR or depth camera produces large data streams over Ethernet or USB (F1-46, F1-51); small single-point ToF sensors often use I2C (F1-47). The interface and packet format are in the datasheet. — source: D4 range-sensor datasheet (none in kit)
  31. F31 (hardware) … che photodiode or an array of single-photon detectors) and an amplifier detect the echo; a comparator marks its arrival; and a time-to-digital converter measures the interval between start and stop, often by combining a fast counter with finer interpolation. — source: D5 AoE 3rd ed. §12.6 (TOC only)
  32. F32 (hardware) In a scanning unit, a motor turns the optics and an encoder (F1-66) reports the angle of each measurement. In a stereo or structured-light camera, two image sensors (F1-68) and often a projector are synchronised, and a processor computes disparities on board. — source: D3 HZ 2nd ed. ch. 6/9/12 (contents only)
  33. F33 (hardware) … safety class under the applicable laser-safety standard (D6) and that class's conditions of use; never look into the emitter, never point it at people's eyes, and never open the housing or modify the optics. L2 learners use one only with a supervising adult. — source: D6 IEC 60825-1:2014 (catalogue entry)
  34. F34 (breaks) A clap goes everywhere; a laser beam goes one way. Kwame hears the nearest echo from any direction. A LiDAR beam is narrow and measures only along its line; a thin object between beams is missed entirely. — source: D1 Thrun et al. (not opened)
  35. F35 (breaks) Sound is slow; light is not. Kwame could time an echo by ear. Light covers a metre and back in under 7 ns (Listing 1), so the sensor needs electronics that time in picoseconds, not a stopwatch. — source: R1 (local run, labs/F1-67)
  36. F36 (breaks) Silence is obvious to Kwame; to a program it is a number. When no echo returns, Kwame knows he heard nothing. A driver must encode "nothing" as some value, and a program that forgets to check treats it as a distance (forensic lab). — source: R4 (local run, labs/F1-67)
  37. F37 (transition) Where you came from: F1-64 (measurement chain, resolution), F1-66 (counting with timers). What carries over: resolution set by a timer's tick; invalid states that must be detected (the decoder's diagonal, here the "no echo" value); sampling a moving world. — source: D1 Thrun et al. (not opened)
  38. F38 (transition) What changes: these sensors are active (they emit light, with safety rules); each reading is a geometric measurement in a frame, not a single number; failures are part of normal operation and come as special values; and data volumes become large. — source: D1 Thrun et al. (not opened)
  39. F39 (lab) Safety. Simulator: none beyond normal computer use. Optional real sensor: see the laser safety box above. — source: S1 guide (local)
