Exam papers of HW102 Transistors to logic gates (level L1–L2, 3 credits), written under owner ruling B4 by the Exam Writer and Lab Engineer pass. Everything here can be answered from chapters F1-09 to F1-15 and their lab listings. The course uses Verilog with Icarus Verilog and Yosys instead of the Nand2Tetris simulator (the verifier's ruling, recorded in the course NOTES). All circuits are simulated only: untested on hardware. Every delay is in the course model's time units (1 unit per gate), not nanoseconds.

How the course is graded

HW102 starts at level L1 (F1-09 and F1-10 are L1 chapters; F1-14 and F1-15 are L2), so owner ruling B6 is applied. The graded parts are the quizzes, the practical and the course project, and each comes back with encouraging written feedback. The midterm and the final are written (ruling B4) but are ungraded practice papers: you sit them to find out what to practise before the practical and the project. Because part of the course is L2, the papers still follow the L2 blueprint rule (at most 15 % Remember).

Quizzes (Q)

Each chapter's "Check yourself" is its quiz: seven or eight questions, with at least one "explain in your own words" and one "apply to a new case", and in every chapter a "predict, then run it" question (guide 11.3). Learners answer in writing after reading the chapter and before its lab, then mark themselves against the chapter's answers. A supervisor or mentor checks the "in your own words" answers against the generic rubric (guide 11.4) and checks that every "predict" answer has the learner's own run next to it. The seven quizzes together are worth 20 % of the course.

  • Quiz 1 — F1-09 Check yourself (nMOS and pMOS rules; the CMOS inverter; floating and short).
  • Quiz 2 — F1-10 Check yourself (series and parallel; NAND and NOR from four transistors; the dual rule).
  • Quiz 3 — F1-11 Check yourself (every gate from NAND; De Morgan; functional completeness; Yosys counts).
  • Quiz 4 — F1-12 Check yourself (multiplexers, decoders, mux trees, design from a description).
  • Quiz 5 — F1-13 Check yourself (binary addition; half and full adders; the width trap).
  • Quiz 6 — F1-14 Check yourself (ripple-carry adder; subtraction; the ALU and its flags; testing).
  • Quiz 7 — F1-15 Check yourself (propagation delay; the 2N + 1 model; glitches; reading too early).

Course project — a tested 16-bit ALU

Weight 30 % (ruling B6). Brief per guide 11.5. The project grows from the steps that the chapters already define: the 8-to-1 multiplexer of the F1-12 lab, the gate-level ALU of the F1-14 lab, the design document of the F1-14 mini-project and the timing report of the F1-15 mini-project.

Goal

Build a structural 16-bit ALU with the course's opcode table and flags, and show with evidence that it is correct, how fast it is in the course's timing model, and how big it is in gates.

Requirements

  • Functional: the eight operations of F1-14 (000 ADD to 111 PASS b) and the flags Z, N, C, V exactly as defined there. The ALU is built from gate primitives, your own full adder and your own mux2 only; behavioural code is allowed only in the testbench's reference model. The width is a parameter, and the same module passes at 4 and at 16 bits.
  • Tests: a reference-model testbench (F1-14, Listing 4 style), exhaustive at 4 bits (2,048 combinations). At 16 bits: at least six edge cases, each with a written reason, plus at least 100,000 pseudo-random vectors from a fixed seed that you state. A planted fault (your choice) that the testbench detects, with its output attached.
  • Timing: the ALU simulated with 1 time unit per gate. Report the worst-case settle time you measured for ADD, a no-carry case and an opcode change; describe the critical path in words; compare with the 2N + 1 adder prediction of F1-15 and explain the difference.
  • Size: the Yosys NAND/NOT cell count of the 16-bit ALU, converted to NAND-equivalents and transistors.
  • Quality: builds with iverilog -g2012 -Wall with no warnings (no implicit wires); every inside wire named; every command reproducible from the report; every number labelled as a model unit, a count, or a measurement in simulation.

Constraints

Hardware: none. The project is simulation only and untested on hardware; the FPGA step in the practical's box is an optional extension and is not marked. Tools: Icarus Verilog and Yosys (the versions recorded in the course labs). Time: about ten hours in all, spread over F1-12 to F1-15. Work alone; you may discuss test ideas, not code.

Deliverables

  1. The ALU source and the testbenches.
  2. The unedited outputs of the 4-bit and 16-bit runs and of the planted-fault run, each with its command and toolchain line.
  3. The design document of the F1-14 mini-project: opcode table, flag definitions, block diagram matching the modules, test strategy (edge cases with reasons, seed, vector count), and one paragraph on what a random test cannot prove.
  4. The timing report of the F1-15 mini-project, adapted to the whole ALU, with the gate count added.
  5. A five-minute demo: run the test, then explain one edge case and the critical path.
