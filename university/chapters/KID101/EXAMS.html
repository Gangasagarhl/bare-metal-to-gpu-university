Note

How feedback is written for an L0 learner. Every marked part gets three sentences, in this order: one thing that was done well (quote it), one thing to try next time (one thing only), and one sentence that names the skill the learner now has ("you can write steps a machine can follow"). Numbers are kept for the record; the learner sees the sentences first.

Final (F) — ungraded practice paper

Time: 60 minutes, with a break allowed. Allowed: the course chapters, your bit board, your paper computer's rule card, pencil and paper. How it is used: you answer first, then you and a grown-up mark it together with the answer key. The points show which parts of the course are solid and which to read again; they are not a grade (ruling B6). Every chapter is in the paper; the forensic question at the end is the kind of detective work the course trained.

Questions

Part A — Steps and recipes (F0-01)

  1. (3 points) A computer is given a recipe with one step missing. What does it do? (a) It works out the missing step and adds it. (b) It stops and asks you what you meant. (c) It does the steps it has, exactly as written, and nothing else.
  2. (6 points) Write four exact steps for pouring cereal into a bowl, for a helper who does only what the words say. Each step must do one thing, say what to use, and say when it is finished. (Hint from F0-01: "one finger from the top" is exact; "enough" is not.)

Part B — Bits and bytes (F0-02)

  1. (4 points) What number is the byte 01100100? Show which place values you added.
  2. (4 points) Show the number 22 as a row of eight switches. Write all eight digits.
  3. (4 points) A row has six switches. Write the six place values from left to right, and the biggest number the row can show.

Part C — Codes (F0-03)

  1. (5 points) Using the letter table printed by Listing 3 of F0-03 (A = 65, B = 66, … Z = 90), decode the message 01000010 01011001 01000101. Show the number of each byte.
  2. (3 points) A friend says: "The byte 66 always means the letter B." Explain in your own words why that is not quite right.

Part D — Sorting (F0-04)

  1. (5 points) Send the cards 4 2 3 1 through the sorting network of Figure 1 in F0-04 (stations: spots 0–1, 2–3, 0–2, 1–3, 1–2; the smaller card goes to the upper spot). Write the row after each of the five stations.
  2. (3 points) How many comparisons does "find the smallest" use for seven cards? Show the sum.

Part E — Inside the box (F0-05)

  1. (4 points) Fill in the computer part for each clue: (i) tiny and the fastest to reach; the chef's hands. (ii) forgets everything when the power goes off; the big pantry. (iii) keeps your files when the computer is off; the warehouse across town. (iv) carries information between all the parts; the corridors.
  2. (5 points) In the toy kitchen, box 0 holds 4, box 1 holds 9 and box 2 holds 0. Priya's recipe is: LOAD_LEFT 0, LOAD_RIGHT 1, STORE_LEFT 2, ADD. Trace the hands and the boxes after every line. What ends up in box 2, what should be there, and what is the fix?

Part F — Inputs and outputs (F0-06)

  1. (4 points) A fan program has the rule "if the temperature is more than 25, fan ON; otherwise fan off" (the numbers are on a made-up scale). For the readings 20, 25, 26 and 30, write the output for each one. Be careful with 25.
  2. (3 points) A drawing program is running and the keyboard works, but the screen shows nothing at all. Which of the three parts (input, processing, output) would you check first, and what is one check you could do?

Part G — Decisions and loops (F0-07)

  1. (6 points) Add 1011 + 0110 with the paper computer's rules (count the 1s in each column, including the carry: 0 → write 0, carry 0; 1 → write 1, carry 0; 2 → write 0, carry 1; 3 → write 1, carry 1). Show every column from right to left, the final carry, the five-switch answer and the answer as an ordinary number.
  2. (4 points) A helper follows these steps: "spoons = 0. While spoons is less than 3: say 'hello'." Does the helper ever stop? Explain why, and write the one step that is missing.

Part H — Bugs (F0-08)

  1. (5 points) A program is meant to print the numbers 1 to 5. It prints 1 2 3 4 and reports exit code 0. Use the detective method: write the symptom, two guesses, the test that decides between them, the most likely root cause, and a test you would keep so the bug cannot come back. Also say why the exit code is 0.

Part I — Networks (F0-09)

  1. (4 points) A message of 26 characters is sent on postcards that hold 8 characters each. (i) How many cards are needed? Show why. (ii) The cards arrive in the order 4, 1, 1, 3. Write what the receiver does, step by step, to get the whole message.
  2. (3 points) Explain in your own words why the receiver cannot notice a missing last card if the cards say only their own number and not "of 4".

Part J — Robots and drones (F0-10)

  1. (4 points) A robot vacuum cleaner must never fall down the stairs. Describe its sense, think and act for this job: name a sensor, the yes-or-no question its program asks, and what its motors do in each case.
  2. (4 points) Kai says: "To save time, my robot should read its wall sensor once at the start and remember the answer for the whole run." Is this a good idea? Say yes or no, give the reason from the control loop, and say what the trace of F0-10's forensic lab showed when a robot did this.

Forensic question — "The helper that stood still" (17 points)

Scenario. For the practical of this course, the university wrote a "literal bean helper": the course's own tiny simulator, like the literal cook of F0-01. Two cups stand on a table, a left cup with 10 beans and an empty right cup. The helper has one hand and does exactly what each written step says. Its steps are: PICK UP ONE BEAN FROM THE LEFT CUP, PUT THE BEAN IN THE RIGHT CUP, IF THE RIGHT CUP HAS n BEANS, STOP, GO BACK TO STEP n and STOP. If a step cannot be done (for example putting down a bean when the hand is empty), the helper does nothing for that step and says so. It gives up after 30 steps. Yara wrote four steps to move exactly five beans into the right cup. The helper ran and ran, and then gave up with only one bean moved.

Questions.

  1. Observation (3 points). How many beans ended in the right cup, and how many should have? Which line of the trace is the first one where "nothing happened", and what reason does the helper give?
  2. Guesses (3 points). Write two different guesses about what is wrong, before you decide.
  3. Root cause (4 points). Which step is wrong? Prove it with lines from the evidence: say why the hand is empty every time step 2 runs again.
  4. Judge the guesses (4 points). One of these guesses is wrong: "the helper's hand is broken", or "the loop goes back to the wrong step". Say which is wrong and point at the evidence that rules it out. Then explain why the helper gave up after 30 steps instead of finishing.
  5. Fix and prevention (3 points). Write the corrected four steps. Then write one thing Yara could do next time, before running, to catch this kind of mistake.

Method hints. Read the trace one line at a time. For each "nothing happened", ask: what must be true for this step to work, and which earlier step should have made it true? Compare where "GO BACK" goes with where the hand gets a new bean.

Practical (P) — 50 %

The skill (course card): write an algorithm another person can follow without asking questions. In this practical, the "other person" is first a grown-up who plays a literal helper, and then the university's literal bean helper, a tiny program that follows your steps word for word.

Time limit: 40 minutes for Part 1 and Part 2 together; a 5-minute break is allowed. Allowed: the course chapters (F0-01, F0-07 and F0-08 are the useful ones), your own notes, pencil and paper; a grown-up may type and run your steps for you but may not change a word. Hardware: none; two cups and some dry beans for Part 1, and a computer with the course's C++ compiler for Part 2 (or a grown-up who runs it).

The task

On the table stand a left cup with 10 dry beans and a right cup. Write steps so that the helper ends with exactly 5 beans in the right cup and then stops. Your steps must work in both of these situations without changing a word:

  • Situation 1: the right cup is empty at the start.
  • Situation 2: the right cup already holds 2 beans at the start.

Part 1 (paper, 15 minutes). Write your steps on paper, numbered. A grown-up plays the helper with real cups and beans, doing only what the words say, without asking anything. Watch, mark any step where the helper got stuck, and fix your steps.

Part 2 (the machine, 25 minutes). Type your steps into the starting file, one step per line, using only the five step shapes the helper understands. Run it in situation 1 and situation 2. Fix and run again until both runs say task passed: yes.

The five step shapes the helper understands (capital letters, exact words; n is a whole number):

StepWhat the helper doesWhen it does nothing
PICK UP ONE BEAN FROM THE LEFT CUPtakes one bean from the left cup into its handthe hand already holds a bean, or the left cup is empty

IF THE RIGHT CUP HAS n BEANS, STOPcounts the right cup; if it has exactly n beans, stops (also IF THE LEFT CUP HAS n BEANS, STOP)the count is different: it goes on to the next step

Any other line is skipped with "I do not understand this step". The helper gives up after 30 steps, so a loop that never ends still shows a short trace. (The counts and the limit are exercise values.)

Course project — "A paper computer that adds" — 30 %

Goal. Build a paper computer that adds two small binary numbers by following written rules, and show that a person who has never seen it can run it and get the right answer every time.

Requirements.

  • Boards: two four-switch boards (place values 8, 4, 2, 1) for the two numbers and one five-switch answer board (16, 8, 4, 2, 1), as in the F0-02 mini-project, with the place values written under every switch.
  • The program: a rule card, copied from the F0-07 worked example, that says what to write and what to carry for 0, 1, 2 or 3 ones in a column, and the loop "for each column from right to left".
  • Correctness: the paper computer gives the right answer for at least five sums chosen by someone else, including 0 + 0 and 15 + 15 (which must give 11110, that is 30).
  • Quality: a stranger can run it from the card alone; the boards are labelled so that a friend can read them (F0-02 rubric); the carry is written above the next column every time.
  • Evidence: a record sheet of every sum run: the two numbers, the answer board, the check in ordinary adding, and a tick or a cross.

Constraints. Paper, pencil, coins or paper squares only; no computer is needed. About four short sessions in all: it starts in F0-02 and ends after F0-07. A grown-up or a friend must be the "stranger" for the demo.

Deliverables. (1) The three boards and the rule card. (2) The record sheet with at least five sums. (3) A short report of five to ten sentences: how the machine works, one mistake you made and how you found it (F0-08), and where the machine stops working (for example 15 + 15 fits, but what about 16?). (4) A two-minute demo where a stranger runs one sum.

Milestones.

  1. After F0-02: the three boards show 5 and 3 and the answer 8 (the F0-02 mini-project rubric, 4 points, counts as this milestone's check).
  2. After F0-07: the rule card is written and the lab "Run the paper computer" is done: five sums right, including 15 + 15 = 11110.
  3. After F0-08: one wrong sum has been debugged with the detective method (or, if none went wrong, one has been made wrong on purpose and found).
  4. End of course: the stranger demo and the report.
