Story hook

Two buildings across town, four kitchen halls in each. For a whole course you passed bowls around round tables, timed the road between the buildings, and wrote the rules for what to do when a hall goes silent. Now the restaurant group gives you the job for real: a service that adds up every hall's numbers and hands the total back to every hall, as fast as the roads allow, every evening, without ever serving a wrong total.

Lindiwe, who runs the group, asks three questions before she signs anything. "Is it right for every order size, not just the round ones? How fast is it compared with the delivery company we could hire instead? And what happens when one hall stops answering in the middle of service?" This handbook is how you answer her with evidence instead of promises.

What you will be able to do

By the final defence (R4) you will have built, measured and defended a multi-node all-reduce. Concretely, you will be able to:

  • Build a chunked ring all-reduce for the GPUs of one node and prove it correct for every message size from 4 bytes to 1 GiB, with a test suite that you have shown to catch known bugs (milestone 1).
  • Extend it into a hierarchical two-node all-reduce over GPU-aware MPI or verbs, correct on equal and unequal layouts (milestone 2).
  • Measure bus-bandwidth curves of your all-reduce and of NCCL or RCCL on the same machines, with GPUDirect RDMA on and off where the setup allows, against targets you fixed before measuring (milestone 3).
  • Predict every curve with the alpha-beta model from measured α and β, and explain each gap between model and measurement (the model-versus-measurement report).
  • Integrate the all-reduce into a training step with overlap, and make the whole job fail cleanly and resume when a rank dies (milestone 4).
  • Defend the work at review gates with a packet of logs that anyone can rerun.

Jargon box
Link (transport interface)
Simple words: the one small function the all-reduce uses to talk to its neighbours, so the same algorithm can run over threads, MPI, GPU copies or RDMA. Analogy: the hatch between two halls: the cooks do not care whether a runner, a conveyor belt or a van carries the bowl through it. Precise: in this project, Link<T>::sendRecv(send, ns, recv, nr) sends ns elements to rank r + 1 and receives nr elements from rank r − 1, returning a status; the ring algorithm calls nothing else. R1 R4
Traffic invariant (ring all-reduce)
Simple words: the exact number of elements each rank must send in one all-reduce; sending more or fewer is a bug even if the answer looks right. Analogy: every hall must pass exactly so many bowls per evening; a hall that passed fewer skipped somebody. Precise: with segments of lengths |segg| (g = 0 … N − 1) and N ≥ 2, rank r sends 2·count − |segr+1| − |segr+2| elements (indices mod N), whose maximum over ranks approaches the bandwidth-optimal 2(N − 1)/N · count. D1 R1
Pre-set target (performance)
Simple words: the percentage of the reference library's speed you promise to reach, written down before you measure. Analogy: naming the time you will beat before the race, not after. Precise: the "X %" of the curriculum's acceptance tests, fixed in the plan and approved at R1; it is never lowered afterwards without the owner's explicit approval and a log entry. C1
Model-versus-measurement report
Simple words: a report that puts what the cost model predicted next to what you measured, size by size, and explains every difference. Analogy: the delivery plan's estimated times next to the drivers' real times, with a reason for every late van. Precise: the MP5 deliverable in which α and β are fitted or measured, predictions for every message size, layout and back-end are tabulated against medians of measured times, the ratio predicted / measured is reported, and each region outside the agreed tolerance has an evidence-backed cause. C2 R5
Aborted collective (buffer state)
Simple words: when an all-reduce is stopped half-way, the data buffers hold a mix of partial sums and must not be used. Analogy: a pot that was half-way through being combined when service stopped: you cook again from the saved ingredients, you do not serve it. Precise: after any rank's collective returns an error, every rank's buffer is treated as undefined; recovery runs a new communicator on inputs saved before the call (or on the last checkpoint). In the starter lab, re-running on the aborted buffers gives values outside the error bound. R3

Layer 1 — the kid explanation

Eight friends each have a list of numbers, and everyone wants the list of totals. Instead of everyone shouting at once, they sit in a circle and pass pieces of their lists to the left, adding as they go, until every piece has been added by everyone; then they pass the finished pieces around once more so that everyone has all of them. When the friends live in two houses, they first add up inside each house, then one friend per piece phones the other house, then each house shares the result. This project is making that game fast, correct for any list length, and safe when one friend falls asleep.

Layer 2 — the student explanation

You already know every part of this project. F8-02 taught the ring all-reduce as a reduce-scatter followed by an all-gather, and F8-05 had you build one with chunking on threads. F8-03 gave you the alpha-beta model, t = α + m/β for one message of m bytes, and the two bandwidths benchmark reports print: algorithm bandwidth S/t and bus bandwidth S/t · 2(N − 1)/N for all-reduce. F8-09 measured a second "node" over TCP, and F8-10 composed a hierarchical all-reduce from MPI communicators. F8-11 overlapped it with a backward pass, and F8-16 made a job survive a lost rank. D1 D3

What is new is integration and evidence. The pieces must become one library with one interface, tested hard enough that a reviewer believes "correct for all sizes", measured against the vendor's library on real hardware with targets fixed in advance, explained with a model, and robust when something breaks. The rubric weighs exactly those four things: correctness 30 %, performance against pre-set targets 25 %, model accuracy and explanation 25 %, robustness 20 %. C2

The starter lab of this handbook gives you the skeleton on CPU threads: the library header with the Link interface, a test suite that covers every remainder of the message length, a mutation check that proves the suite catches four known bugs, fault tests with a timeout and abort, the same ring over MPI processes, a bandwidth harness, and an alpha-beta fit that prints model against measurement. Everything you build on GPUs plugs into that skeleton.

The cost models you will defend

For a flat ring over P ranks on equal links, the model of F8-03 is t = 2(P − 1)(α + S/(Pβ)). The chunked implementations of this project send c chunks per segment, so the starter lab fits the form t = m·α + b/β with m = 2(N − 1)c messages and b = 2(N − 1)/N · S bytes per rank. The hierarchical all-reduce of F8-10 adds an intra-node term with (αintra, βintra) and an inter-node term with (αinter, βinter) shared by the G flows that leave each node; whether the two terms add or overlap depends on your pipelining. D1 D2 R5 Your report states which form it uses, where its parameters came from, and how far each measured point lies from it.

How the hardware actually does it

On real hardware, the transports under the Link are GPU copies over the node's links (PCIe, NVLink or xGMI, F8-04), a reduction kernel per chunk, and an RDMA network between nodes in which the NIC reads and writes registered memory without the remote CPU, directly from GPU memory when GPUDirect RDMA (or the AMD equivalent) is available, otherwise through host staging (F8-08, F8-09). Worked example


(a) The traffic invariant for N = 4, count = 7


Segments are [g·7/4, (g + 1)·7/4) with integer division: [0, 1), [1, 3), [3, 5), [5, 7), lengths 1, 2, 2, 2. Rank r sends every segment but r + 1 in the reduce-scatter and every segment but r + 2 in the all-gather. So rank 0 sends 14 − |seg 1| − |seg 2| = 14 − 2 − 2 = 10 elements; rank 1 sends 14 − 2 − 2 = 10; rank 2 sends 14 − 2 − 1 = 11; rank 3 sends 14 − 1 − 2 = 11. The bandwidth-optimal figure 2(N − 1)/N · count is 10.5; the busiest rank sends 11 because segments cannot be split into half elements. D1 The suite checks this number exactly for every rank and case; that check, not the values check, is what first caught three of the four mutants in Listing 2's run. R2


(b) A prediction from the fitted model, checked against the measurement


The recorded fit (Lab step 5) gave, for N = 4 threads, α = 33.10 µs per message and β = 2.875 GB/s per rank. For S = 64 MiB = 67,108,864 bytes and chunks of 16,384 floats, each segment of 16 MiB has 256 chunks, so m = 2 · 3 · 256 = 1,536 messages and b = 2 · 3/4 · 67,108,864 = 100,663,296 bytes. Then m·α = 1,536 × 33.10 ≈ 50,842 µs and b/β = 100,663,296 / 2,875 bytes per µs ≈ 35,013 µs, so the model predicts about 85,855 µs; fit.out prints 85,866 µs (it uses the unrounded parameters). The measured median was 99,130 µs: the model is 13 % low at this size. R5 Two things stand out. First, the per-message term is larger than the per-byte term at 64 MiB: on threads every chunk pays a lock, a wake-up and a copy into a new vector, so the "α" fitted here is mostly synchronisation, and larger chunks should help; that is a hypothesis for you to test, not a conclusion. Second, one α and one β cannot capture a machine whose caches hold small messages and not large ones; a model with a second regime, or a fit restricted to large sizes, is the honest next step.


The fit also shows why one run is not evidence. Two trial runs of the same harness made earlier on the same container, while other jobs were running (not kept as lab records), gave fitted α values for N = 2 about ten times apart. The recorded run's median errors of 0.06 and 0.04 are the best of the three, not a property of the model. Your report must repeat each curve and show the spread.


(c) Why the float results differ from MPI_Allreduce and still pass


Listing 4's run reports 0 float values that differ in bits from MPI_Allreduce on 2 processes, but 1,461,849 of 6,731,058 on 3 and 3,148,852 of 8,974,744 on 4, while every case passes. R4 With two operands there is one addition and a + b = b + a exactly in IEEE arithmetic; with three or more the ring and the library add in different orders, and floating-point addition is not associative. The suite therefore checks floats against a bound, |error| ≤ (N − 1)·u·Σ|xi| with u = 2−24, and demands bit-identical results across ranks of one algorithm, not equality with another algorithm. D6 The largest ratio of error to bound in the suite was 1.000 for N = 2 (one rounding can reach the bound) and fell to 0.527 for N = 8. R1

Where the analogy breaks


    

  • Halls passing bowls wait for each other by looking; computers wait on queues, flags and network completions, and a wait without a deadline lasts forever. Nobody "notices" a silent hall unless you program the timeout.
    • 

  • Soup added in a different order tastes the same; floating-point numbers added in a different order can differ in the last bits. "Same total" means bit-identical only for the same algorithm on the same layout.
    • 

  • A road has one speed; real links have a fixed cost per message (α) and a cost per byte (β), and both depend on who else uses the link. The CPU threads of the starter lab share caches and memory in ways no two GPUs do, so their α and β say nothing about GPUs.
    •

Safety
Read the university's safety section before any hardware step. Run GPU and network experiments only on machines whose data you do not need: driver crashes, hangs and forced resets are normal while learning, and long benchmarks stress power and cooling. C1 Real-hardware steps (milestones 1–4 on GPUs, any change to drivers, kernel modules, NIC or switch settings, and fault injection that kills processes on shared nodes) follow the hardware owner's supervision rules: written permission for the machines and the time window, no configuration change without the owner's approval, the owner's procedure for physical access (never open a machine or recable a network yourself unless those rules say so), and a cost limit for cloud instances agreed in advance. Summary


    

  • MP5 integrates DG401–DG403 into one all-reduce library, single node then two nodes, measured against NCCL/RCCL and defended at gates R1, R2 and R4.
    • 

  • Correctness "for all sizes" means every remainder of count modulo N, checked by values, bit-identity and the exact traffic invariant, and a suite that is shown to catch known bugs.
    • 

  • Targets are fixed at R1 before measuring and never lowered without the owner's approval; curves follow the protocol of warm-up and medians, on the same machines as the reference.
    • 

  • The model-versus-measurement report fits or measures α and β, predicts every curve, and explains every gap; one run is not evidence.
    • 

  • Robustness means deadlines on every wait, an abort that reaches every rank, and recovery from saved inputs or checkpoints, never from aborted buffers.
    • 

  • The starter lab ran on CPU threads and MPI processes only; everything on GPUs and networks is untested in this build.

  2. Segments [g·5/3, (g + 1)·5/3): [0, 1), [1, 3), [3, 5), lengths 1, 2, 2. Rank r sends 10 − |seg r+1| − |seg r+2|: rank 0: 10 − 2 − 2 = 6; rank 1: 10 − 2 − 1 = 7; rank 2: 10 − 1 − 2 = 7. Ranks 1 and 2 are busiest (7; the optimum 2·2/3·5 ≈ 6.7).

Forensic lab answer key


How the evidence was produced. mp5_forensic.cpp runs the ring of mp5_allreduce.h compiled with Mutant::split_floor ("build B"): segment boundaries are g·⌊count/N⌋, so the last count mod N elements belong to no segment. R6


    

  1. Every count divisible by 4 passes (1,024, 4,096, 65,536, 1,000); counts with remainder 1, 2, 3 fail with exactly 1, 2, 3 wrong elements (1,001, 1,002, 1,003, 4,099); count 3 has all 3 wrong.
    2. 

  2. The wrong elements are the last count mod 4 indices (1,000–1,002; 4,096–4,098; 0–2 for count 3). Rank 0 holds 122 at index 1,000 where 1,220 is expected: 122 is exactly rank 0's own input, (0 + 1)·(1,000 mod 251 − 125) = 247 − 125 = 122. No step ever touched those elements.
    3. 

  3. Hypothesis A: those elements are reduced but the all-gather does not deliver them (then rank 0 would hold a partial or full sum on some rank). Hypothesis B: they are never part of any segment (then every rank holds its own input). The values (each rank's own input) and the unchanged "elements sent" for 1,000 to 1,003 (1,500 each, the figure for 1,000) support B: the extra elements are never sent at all.
    4. 

  4. Root cause: segment() with Mutant::split_floor uses len = count / N and boundaries g·len, so ⌊count/N⌋·N < count elements are covered. Fix: boundaries g·count/N and (g + 1)·count/N, as in the real code, which distributes the remainder.
    5. 

  5. The traffic invariant: for count 1,003 each rank must send 2·1,003 − |seg r+1| − |seg r+2| = 1,504 or 1,505 elements (segments of 250 and 251), but build B sent 1,500. That check would fail even if the values happened to be right.
    6. 



Wrong answers and why. "A race condition": the failures are deterministic and depend only on count mod N. "Float rounding": the data are integers and the error is a factor of 10. "The all-gather is short": then sizes divisible by 4 would also fail.
